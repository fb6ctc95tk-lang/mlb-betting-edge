## Preamble

The First Five Innings (F5) moneyline is a partial-game market. It asks one question: which team leads — or is tied — after five complete innings of play? The F5 market strips the full-game moneyline of its most variable elements — bullpen performance, pinch-hitting, and late-game managerial adjustment — and concentrates analytical attention on the factor that most reliably governs the first half of any baseball game: starting pitcher quality.

This playbook establishes the analytical methodology the Oracle uses when evaluating MLB First Five Innings moneyline opportunities. It defines what makes the F5 market analytically distinct from the full-game moneyline, what factors are most material within the five-inning window, and how those factors should be weighed, documented, and translated into a verdict.

This document is a methodology document. It teaches how the Oracle thinks about F5 decisions. It does not contain current team information, current player data, seasonal statistics, historical trend databases, or sportsbook-specific guidance. Those are transient. The analytical principles that govern sound F5 evaluation are durable.

This playbook derives its authority from the Oracle Constitution (Level 1) and the Oracle Evaluation Standard (Level 2). It is consistent with and informed by the Oracle Audit Standard, Oracle Tracker Standard, and Oracle Playbook Standard. It may expand upon higher-level documents by adding market-specific detail, procedure, and analytical criteria. It may not contradict them.

## Article II — Scope

### Section 2.1 — Market Definition

This playbook governs the evaluation of First Five Innings (F5) moneyline markets in Major League Baseball. An F5 moneyline bet resolves based on the score at the conclusion of five complete innings of play. The home team must complete its half of the fifth inning for the bet to resolve unless the home team is leading after the top of the fifth, in which case the market resolves per standard action rules applicable at the time of evaluation.

If a game is suspended, called, or cannot complete the minimum innings required for F5 action — per the house rules governing the bet at the time of evaluation — the F5 moneyline is treated as a Void outcome for tracker purposes.

This playbook does not govern:

- Full-game moneyline bets
- Run line bets (spread-adjusted moneylines, full-game or F5)
- Game totals or first-five-innings totals
- Team totals
- Player or pitching props
- Any non-MLB market

### Section 2.2 — What "First Five Innings" Means Analytically

The five-inning scope is not simply a shortened version of the full game. It is a structurally distinct analytical object with its own internal logic.

Within a typical five-inning window:

- Each lineup sees the starting pitcher approximately twice through the order. The third time through is possible in the fifth inning depending on lineup position and inning length, but is not guaranteed.
- The starting pitcher is expected to be the pitcher of record for all or nearly all of the F5 window, except in cases of early hook tendencies, documented pitch count limits, or opener deployments.
- The bullpen, while technically capable of appearing, plays a far smaller role in F5 outcomes than in full-game outcomes. Bullpen analysis is correspondingly reduced in scope compared to the full-game moneyline.
- There are no extra innings within the F5 window. A tie after five complete innings is a push in most F5 market formats, not a continuation to a winner.
- Late-game managerial decisions — defensive substitutions, pinch-hitting, matchup-driven pitcher changes — are mostly outside the F5 window.

Expressed in terms of game action, the F5 market evaluates approximately the first 18–21 defensive outs — the outs recorded by each team's pitching across the five-inning analytical window. This narrower frame changes the relative importance of the available pregame evidence. Factors that require full-game accumulation to become analytically visible — bullpen performance across multiple high-leverage appearances, lineup restructuring through late-inning pinch-hitting, and extra-inning scenarios — either do not occur within the F5 window or appear only at the margins. The analytical emphasis falls on what is identifiable, documentable, and confirmable about the first half of the game: primarily the starting pitching matchup, the platoon dynamics within the first two times through the order, and the lineup's top-of-order offensive composition.

The Oracle evaluates F5 opportunities as a question of which team is more likely to lead, or push, after five complete innings — not which team is more likely to win the game.

## Article III — F5 Market Philosophy

### Section 3.1 — The F5 Is a Probability Bet on a Partial-Game Outcome

An F5 moneyline bet is, at its foundation, a statement about probability. The Oracle is not predicting a specific score or asserting a certain outcome. It is asserting that one side is more likely than the market price implies to be leading — or pushing — at the five-inning mark, in a specific, documentable way.

This distinction shapes every aspect of the F5 evaluation. The Oracle is not asking "who will win this game?" It is asking: "Does this price fairly compensate for the probability that this team is ahead after five innings, given what I know about how this game is likely to develop in its first half?"

The five-inning constraint changes what matters. Factors that dominate full-game probability — bullpen depth, late-game roster decisions, extra-inning performance — are substantially reduced in scope or eliminated entirely. The F5 market isolates the pregame analytical factors that most directly govern the first half of a baseball game.

### Section 3.2 — Starting Pitcher Quality Is the Primary Variable

In the full-game moneyline, starting pitcher quality is the most important single analytical input — but it competes in analytical weight with bullpen performance, lineup depth, and late-game context. In the F5 market, starting pitcher quality is the primary variable with a structural advantage that does not exist in the full-game market.

The reason is structural: within five innings, the starting pitcher accounts for all or nearly all of one team's pitching. An analytical advantage in starting pitcher quality maps more directly to an F5 advantage than to a full-game advantage, because the variable that dilutes the starting pitcher's contribution — the bullpen's eventual involvement — is mostly outside the five-inning window.

This does not mean F5 evaluation reduces to SP quality comparison alone. It means SP quality comparison is the analytical foundation on which all other factors are layered. An F5 recommendation built without a well-grounded SP assessment is structurally incomplete.

### Section 3.3 — The Oracle's Relationship to the F5 Market Price

The F5 market price encodes the market's collective assessment of the probability that one side leads after five innings. That market assessment incorporates most publicly available information efficiently. The Oracle's task is to form its own five-inning probability estimate independently and identify specific, documentable reasons why its estimate may differ meaningfully from what the market has priced.

The Oracle does not recommend an F5 position simply because it has a directional view about which team is better or which pitcher is more talented. It must be able to articulate specifically what the market has mispriced in the five-inning window and why — and the case must survive a genuine comparison against the market price.

### Section 3.4 — Probability Estimation, Not Preference

The Oracle's analytical objective in the F5 market is to estimate the probability that each side leads after five innings as accurately as the available evidence allows — not to identify the team it prefers, respects, or expects to win the game.

This is especially important in the F5 context because the partial-game scope means that a team's overall strength may not fully translate into F5 advantages. A team with a superior bullpen, excellent late-game offense, or a strong pinch-hitting roster may have full-game advantages that do not appear in the five-inning window. The Oracle's F5 analysis is calibrated to the market being evaluated, not to the Oracle's general assessment of team quality.

When assessing evidence, the Oracle explicitly calibrates its confidence based on the quality and completeness of what it has found. Evidence that is confirmed, specific, and directly relevant to the five-inning outcome warrants higher confidence. Evidence that is projected, general, or derived from full-game performance metrics warrants lower confidence — and the probability estimate must reflect that uncertainty.

### Section 3.5 — The Nature of F5 Uncertainty

The F5 market involves genuine uncertainty. Five innings of baseball can produce outcomes driven by factors not fully visible in pregame analysis — a batter executing against type, a starting pitcher requiring unusual early workload through inefficiency, an unexpected early injury, or weather changes within the early innings.

The Oracle's goal is not to predict outcomes with certainty. It is to estimate five-inning probability accurately and consistently. Applied over a meaningful sample, disciplined F5 evaluation should produce a record where the Oracle's analytical basis for its recommendations — the reasons it identified an edge — can be honestly evaluated against what the outcomes show.

## Article IV — When the F5 Market Is Appropriate

The F5 market is the right vehicle when all of the following conditions hold:

### Section 4.1 — The Thesis Centers on the Starting Pitching Matchup

The Oracle's directional conviction stems primarily from a specific, documentable quality or matchup advantage in the starting pitching comparison. The five-inning window gives the starting pitcher substantial analytical weight, and a thesis that does not rest on a clearly assessed SP matchup is likely engaging the F5 market for the wrong reason.

### Section 4.2 — The Bullpen Case Is Neutral or Uncertain

The Oracle either has limited conviction about the full-game bullpen comparison, or the bullpen cases for both teams are approximately balanced. If the Oracle's primary analytical conviction is about bullpen quality differences rather than starting pitcher differences, the F5 market is not where that conviction is best expressed.

### Section 4.3 — The Full-Game Case Is Less Clear

The Oracle has meaningful conviction about the five-inning picture but less conviction about the complete game outcome — due to bullpen uncertainty, scheduling factors that affect the late game, or lineup changes that increase full-game uncertainty. The F5 is analytically more honest than forcing a full-game position when the Oracle's clarity is primarily about the first half.

### Section 4.4 — The Price Is Acceptable

The F5 odds available on the recommended side are consistent with the Oracle's probability estimate for the five-inning outcome. The Oracle can articulate why the price is fair or favorable relative to its five-inning analysis. See Article IX for the price evaluation framework.

## Article V — When Another Market Is Preferable

The Oracle must actively consider alternative markets before committing to an F5 recommendation. The following are common situations where another market better expresses the thesis.

### Section 5.1 — Prefer the Full-Game Moneyline When

- The Oracle has high conviction about the complete game — including the bullpen comparison and the late-game outcome — and the full-game moneyline price is reasonable relative to that full-game conviction.
- The Oracle's analytical advantage extends meaningfully beyond the five-inning window. Betting the F5 when the full-game case is stronger may underexpress the thesis.
- One team has a clear and documentable bullpen advantage the Oracle expects to matter in the late innings, and the full-game price reflects that advantage at acceptable value.

The full-game moneyline is preferred when the Oracle's conviction covers the entire game, not just the first half. An Oracle that reflexively bets F5 when it has a full-game thesis is using the wrong market.

### Section 5.2 — Prefer the Game Total When

- The Oracle's primary conviction is about the scoring environment in the first five innings — specifically, that both starters will suppress run-scoring, or that both lineups will produce offense effectively — rather than which team is more likely to lead.
- An F5 total is available and better captures the Oracle's thesis about the five-inning run environment than an F5 moneyline.
- The Oracle has no meaningful directional conviction between the two teams over the first five innings but has strong conviction about the aggregate five-inning scoring level.

### Section 5.3 — Prefer the Run Line When

- The Oracle's F5 thesis includes a specific, documentable basis for believing the advantaged team will not merely lead after five innings, but will lead by a meaningful margin — and an F5 run line is available at an attractive price.
- The SP quality advantage is so pronounced that the directional thesis also supports a margin thesis, and the F5 run line price represents additional value the moneyline does not.

The F5 run line requires the Oracle to be right about both direction and margin. It is only preferable when both elements of the thesis are well-supported within the five-inning window.

### Section 5.4 — Pass When No Market Is Superior

If the Oracle cannot identify a market that efficiently expresses its analytical position at an acceptable price, the correct outcome is a pass. The absence of a cleanly expressible thesis is a pass condition, not an invitation to search for the least-bad market.

## Article VI — Core Evaluation Categories

The following categories define the specific analytical factors the Oracle must assess when evaluating an F5 opportunity. These categories provide the market-specific content that fills the Evaluation Standard's Handicap Quality and Game Selection categories for F5 decisions.

Each category must be explicitly addressed in the pregame documentation. The Oracle does not assume a factor is neutral without assessing it.

---

### Section 6.1 — Starting Pitcher Quality, Matchup, and Durability

**Why it matters:**

The starting pitcher is the dominant analytical variable in the F5 market in a way that has no full-game equivalent. In a full-game moneyline, a starting pitcher's quality advantage must persist through the bullpen handoff to fully determine the outcome. In the F5 market, the starting pitcher is expected to be the pitcher of record for most or all of the five-inning window. A genuine SP quality advantage translates more directly — and more completely — into F5 edge than into full-game edge.

Durability is an F5-specific dimension of SP analysis that does not carry the same weight in the full-game moneyline. A starting pitcher who is on a documented pitch count, who has a pattern of early-inning struggles, or who frequently requires an early hook due to injury management is an F5 risk that would be resolved in a full-game context by the bullpen taking over. In the F5 market, that early hook directly reduces the anticipated SP advantage.

Starting pitcher assessment in the F5 context therefore requires two distinct evaluations that must not be conflated. **Quality** addresses how effective the pitcher is when pitching — his stuff, command, matchup profile, and likelihood of retiring opposing hitters through the five-inning window. **Durability** addresses whether the pitcher will actually cover the analytical window — his pitch efficiency patterns, historical workload data, and known organizational or health constraints that may shorten his appearance.

Pitch count efficiency is the primary durability signal. A pitcher who consistently requires high pitch counts to navigate each inning — due to deep counts, baserunner management, or poor command — may exhaust his expected window before completing five innings, even when performing at his quality baseline. Conversely, a pitcher with a history of efficient outings who records outs quickly and rarely faces high-leverage baserunner situations is more reliable as an F5 coverage asset. Quality and efficiency are independent variables: a pitcher can be highly effective when he is pitching while simultaneously being inefficient in how he reaches five innings. The Oracle assesses both.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- The relative quality of the two starting pitchers in this specific context: documented form, health, relevant splits, and five-inning track record — not career reputation.
- Pitcher-lineup matchup for the first two times through the order: whether either starter has a documented platoon, velocity, or approach advantage against the opposing lineup's characteristics.
- **Five-inning track record and pitch efficiency:** A pitcher who typically requires high pitch counts to get through five innings carries F5-specific durability risk regardless of overall quality. Pitch efficiency — the number of pitches required per out or per inning — directly affects how long the SP is expected to cover the F5 window. A pitcher averaging deep pitch counts per inning will frequently reach his organizational or in-game threshold before the fifth inning is complete. Documented patterns of third- or fourth-inning struggles, regardless of their cause, also signal elevated F5 window risk that must be assessed independently of the pitcher's full-game quality profile.
- **Early hook risk:** Is there a documented pattern, injury concern, or pitch count limit that makes it plausible this pitcher does not complete five innings? Early hook risk is an F5 consideration that would not appear in a full-game analysis with the same analytical weight.
- Recent workload and rest within the F5 context: short rest or accumulated workload may affect first-inning performance, where the SP sets the early-inning tone before any bullpen option is relevant.
- Confirmation status: a pitching matchup thesis depends on confirmed starter information. A projected starter who has not been officially confirmed is a risk factor, not a confirmed analytical input.

**Common mistakes:**

- Assuming a high-quality SP will be available and effective for all five innings without assessing pitch efficiency or durability history.
- Treating career ERA or reputation as sufficient evidence of current quality, particularly for pitchers with documented recent performance changes.
- Failing to assess early-inning performance patterns independently of overall quality, especially for pitchers known to struggle in the first or second inning.
- Ignoring confirmed pitch count limits or injury management protocols that reduce a SP's five-inning durability.
- Building a five-inning thesis around a starter whose analytical profile is constructed primarily from deep-game or high-inning performance data.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Starting pitcher quality interacts directly with Platoon Advantages and Times Through the Order (Section 6.2) — the value of a SP's approach and stuff depends on how the opposing lineup matches up in TTO 1 and TTO 2. It also interacts with Bullpen and Early Hook Considerations (Section 6.3), because durability concerns introduce bullpen relevance even within the F5 window. Early hook risk cannot be assessed without considering what follows if the SP is removed.

### Section 6.2 — Platoon Advantages and Times Through the Order

**Why it matters:**

In the full-game moneyline, platoon advantages in the SP-versus-lineup matchup are one of many analytical inputs spread across nine innings. In the F5 market, the entire five-inning window is the matchup window. There is no second phase of the game where the lineup adjusts via pinch-hitting, nor a bullpen that neutralizes a platoon-unfavorable matchup by deploying a handedness-specific specialist. The platoon dynamics between each starting pitcher and the opposing lineup play out in full — and without relief.

Times Through the Order (TTO) is a directly relevant analytical concept in F5 evaluation. The well-documented TTO penalty — where opposing hitters gain a measurable advantage the second and third time they face a starting pitcher in a game — shapes the five-inning window in ways that the full-game moneyline can partially absorb through bullpen substitutions. In most F5 windows, the opposing lineup will see the SP through TTO 2 and potentially into TTO 3. Pitchers with a pronounced TTO 2 decline carry elevated risk in the later portion of the five-inning window.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- The handedness split of the opposing lineup relative to the SP's documented platoon performance. A SP with a significant platoon advantage against the opposing lineup's dominant handedness has an F5-specific edge that may not be fully priced into the market.
- Whether the SP's documented quality profile holds across TTO 1 and TTO 2. Pitchers who are materially more effective in TTO 1 than TTO 2 may lose their advantage in the fourth and fifth innings when the opposing lineup has seen them once.
- Whether the SP's pitch mix or approach effectiveness tends to degrade with repetition in a single game. Pitchers heavily reliant on a single pitch that opposing hitters adjust to are at greater TTO 2 risk.
- The opposing lineup's documented ability to perform against the SP's specific handedness and pitch mix in the first two times through the order — not just the lineup's general offensive quality.
- Whether the lineup's most productive hitters are concentrated at the top of the order or distributed throughout, which determines how the TTO 2 leverage concentrates in the fourth and fifth innings.

**Common mistakes:**

- Ignoring TTO dynamics entirely in the F5 context and treating the SP's performance as uniform across all five innings.
- Assuming a SP's platoon advantage fully translates without assessing that lineup's specific handedness composition and the quality of hitters on the unfavorable side of the platoon split.
- Treating a platoon advantage as a decisive factor without considering whether the opposing lineup has internal balance — a right-handed SP facing a majority left-handed lineup gains less platoon advantage if those left-handed hitters are the lineup's best producers.
- Ignoring that the lineup's top-of-order hitters may arrive in TTO 2 during the critical fourth and fifth innings, increasing the importance of TTO 2 performance for the specific hitters most likely to produce runs.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Platoon advantages amplify or reduce the Starting Pitcher Quality advantage assessed in Section 6.1. A SP with marginal overall quality but a strong platoon advantage against the opposing lineup's composition may be a stronger F5 play than a SP with a general quality advantage but no meaningful platoon edge. TTO dynamics interact with Early Lineup Strength (Section 6.4) — the lineup's top producers arriving in TTO 2 within the F5 window determines where the most leverage-sensitive matchups occur.

### Section 6.3 — Bullpen and Early Hook Considerations

**Why it matters:**

In the full-game moneyline, bullpen quality is the second most important analytical category after starting pitching. In the F5 market, the bullpen's role is substantially reduced — but it is not eliminated. Two specific scenarios bring bullpen relevance into the F5 window: early hook risk and scheduled opener deployments.

When a SP faces an early hook — whether due to documented pitch count limits, an injury management protocol, or a persistent pattern of early-inning inefficiency — the bullpen will cover some portion of the F5 window. In those cases, the quality of relief coverage within the five innings becomes analytically relevant to the F5 outcome. This is not a full-game bullpen analysis; it is a targeted assessment of what follows an early hook within the five-inning scope.

Opener deployments present the clearest case. When a team schedules an opener rather than a traditional starting pitcher, the primary pitcher for the first inning or two is a reliever, not the analyst's conventional "starting pitcher." The bulk pitcher who follows is responsible for most of the F5 innings. In this scenario, F5 analysis must treat the opener game as a bullpen-dominant early-inning context and assess the full sequence of what covers the five-inning window.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- Is either SP on a documented pitch count limit or injury management protocol that makes a complete five-inning start uncertain?
- Does either SP have a recent pattern of early removal — not by design, but due to inefficiency or poor early-inning performance — that creates realistic bullpen exposure within the F5 window?
- Is either team using an opener? If so, what does the first-inning coverage look like, and what is the quality of the primary pitcher who follows and covers the bulk of the five innings?
- If early hook risk exists for one SP, what is the quality of the short-relief coverage that would follow? The Oracle does not assume the replacement is analytically neutral.
- Is the early hook risk symmetric — does it affect both sides equally — or one-sided, creating an analytical asymmetry specific to one team's F5 window?

**Common mistakes:**

- Dismissing bullpen considerations entirely in the F5 context without assessing early hook risk for each SP.
- Failing to identify documented pitch count limits that have been publicly announced, which are confirmed evidence, not projected information.
- Treating opener deployments as equivalent to traditional SP deployments without acknowledging the structural difference in F5 pitching coverage.
- Overcorrecting and applying full-game bullpen depth analysis to the F5 context when there is no specific reason to believe the F5 window involves meaningful bullpen exposure.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Bullpen and Early Hook considerations are directly downstream of Starting Pitcher Quality (Section 6.1). An SP durability concern that creates early hook risk immediately introduces bullpen relevance that would not otherwise exist in the F5 context. Market Context and Pricing (Section 6.6) must reflect any asymmetric early hook risk — if one side's F5 window involves a less-certain starting pitcher scenario, the market may or may not have priced that uncertainty accurately.

**Managerial tendencies and the F5 window:**

A manager's characteristic approach to pitcher usage materially affects whether the starting pitcher actually covers the expected five-inning analytical window. These tendencies are not speculative — they reflect documented patterns in how a given manager deploys his pitching staff, and they are often identifiable from public records before first pitch.

Relevant managerial factors include:

- **Early hook tendencies:** Managers differ in their tolerance for a struggling starter. A manager with a documented short leash may remove a pitcher laboring through the second or third inning before he has had the opportunity to settle; a manager who allows starters to work through early difficulty introduces less F5 window uncertainty for the same pitcher under the same conditions.
- **Pitch count management:** Some managers operate within organizational pitch count frameworks regardless of in-game performance. A starter who reaches a documented inning or pitch threshold — even while pitching effectively — faces removal that compresses the analytical window.
- **Opener and bulk pitcher usage:** A manager whose organization regularly deploys openers presents a structurally different F5 pitching sequence than one who consistently starts a traditional starter. Where opener usage is a documented organizational or game-specific decision, the F5 analysis shifts from SP quality to pitching sequence coverage.
- **In-game platoon substitutions within the F5 window:** A manager who predictably deploys platoon-driven pinch-hitters in the fourth or fifth inning against a specific handedness may alter the offensive composition the opposing pitcher faces in TTO 2. Where such substitutions are documentable from organizational tendencies, they are an F5 lineup consideration.

The Oracle treats managerial tendencies as a contextual modifier on the starting pitcher durability assessment. A SP with strong five-inning quality working under a manager with a documented aggressive early-hook pattern carries more F5 window uncertainty than the same pitcher under a manager who consistently allows starters to work through early difficulty.

**Organizational deployment strategies:**

Beyond individual managerial tendencies, some organizations adopt pitching deployment philosophies that systematically affect how the five-inning window is staffed. These are organizational decisions — reflecting roster construction, workload philosophy, and competitive strategy — that produce identifiable patterns across games and seasons.

- **Conservative workload management:** Organizations that deliberately limit starting pitcher pitch counts and innings — particularly for younger pitchers or pitchers on structured return programs — may consistently produce F5 windows where the starting pitcher is expected to be removed at or before a specific inning threshold, regardless of in-game performance.
- **Aggressive early bullpen planning:** Some organizations build their pitching strategy around shorter SP appearances backed by specialized relief sequences. In these cases, the F5 window may involve two or three distinct pitchers, which changes the analytical emphasis from SP quality to the quality of the expected pitching sequence through five innings.
- **Systematic opener programs:** Organizations that regularly deploy openers as a strategic default — not as a one-off game decision — create a predictable class of games where traditional SP-centered F5 analysis does not apply. The Oracle's F5 analysis for an organizational opener deployment shifts from SP quality assessment to pitching sequence assessment: who covers which innings, and what is the aggregate quality of the expected sequence through the five-inning window?

Organizational deployment strategies are identifiable from public information before first pitch. Where an organization has a documented pattern that affects the F5 pitching sequence, the Oracle treats that pattern as a confirmed analytical input — not a speculation — and adjusts its coverage and durability assessment accordingly.

### Section 6.4 — Early Lineup Strength and Offensive Context

**Why it matters:**

In the full-game moneyline, lineup strength is assessed across the entire batting order and across nine or more innings. In the F5 market, the lineup analysis is more concentrated. Each team's lineup sees the opposing pitcher approximately twice through the order, which means the top of the lineup — the first three to five hitters — receives disproportionate analytical weight relative to the bottom of the order.

A team with its strongest offensive production concentrated in the top of the order produces more F5 offense than its aggregate lineup quality implies. A team whose offensive production is distributed evenly through the lineup, or concentrated in the middle and bottom, may be more effective over nine innings than over five. Lineup structure — not just lineup quality — is an F5-specific analytical input.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- Confirmed lineup construction, with specific attention to the top three to five hitters who will see the opposing SP most frequently within the five-inning window.
- Any confirmed absences that affect the top of the lineup specifically — the impact is more concentrated in F5 if the absence falls near the top of the batting order.
- Whether either lineup has a documentable early-count aggressiveness or patience profile that interacts with the opposing SP's pitch efficiency. A lineup that consistently forces early pitch counts can shorten the opposing SP's effective F5 window through accumulated inning difficulty.
- Platoon composition of the top four to five hitters relative to the opposing SP's platoon profile — these are the hitters most likely to appear in TTO 2 during the fourth and fifth innings, where the matchup reaches its highest leverage within the F5 window.
- Whether either lineup's offensive profile — fly-ball-heavy, contact-oriented, high-strikeout — interacts with the park or weather context in a way that is specifically relevant to early-inning scoring.

**Common mistakes:**

- Treating lineup analysis in the F5 context identically to full-game lineup analysis, ignoring the top-of-order concentration effect.
- Failing to adjust lineup analysis for confirmed absences when those absences specifically affect the top of the batting order.
- Overweighting aggregate offensive metrics without accounting for the differential impact of top-of-order versus bottom-of-order production in the five-inning window.
- Using recent full-game offensive production as the primary basis for F5 lineup assessment without filtering that production for the lineup's structure and top-of-order quality.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Early lineup strength is the offensive complement to the SP and platoon analysis in Sections 6.1 and 6.2. The top-of-order focus reinforces the importance of platoon matchups in TTO 1 and TTO 2. Lineup strength also interacts with Situational and Environmental Factors (Section 6.5) — park factors and weather conditions affect the run-scoring environment, and their impact is amplified or reduced by the type of lineup each team fields.

### Section 6.5 — Situational and Environmental Factors

**Why it matters:**

Park factors, weather, and scheduling context are valid analytical inputs in the F5 market, as in the full-game moneyline. Their relevance within the five-inning window differs in some dimensions from their full-game relevance. Weather conditions that might build throughout a game are more stable in the early innings. Scheduling factors that affect the late game — bullpen conservation, roster management for tomorrow's game — are only marginally relevant to the F5 window. Environmental factors that directly affect early-inning pitching and hitting conditions are the most F5-relevant situational inputs.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Park factors:** Does the venue affect the early-inning run environment in ways that interact with the SP matchup and lineup analysis? Extreme pitcher-friendly or hitter-friendly parks may suppress or amplify F5 scoring independent of the pitching matchup.
- **Weather:** Wind direction and speed, temperature, and humidity conditions at game time. Weather is more F5-stable than it may be late in a game. Cold temperatures suppressing offense are a first-inning-relevant factor. Significant wind blowing out may increase early-inning fly-ball carry.
- **Scheduling context within the F5 window:** While late-game scheduling effects are less material in the F5 context, unusual scheduling circumstances that affect a SP's preparation or first-inning readiness may be worth noting as a supporting factor.
- **Precipitation and suspension risk:** An outdoor game with genuine early precipitation risk creates F5 Void risk per Section 2.1. This is an F5-specific consideration — not an analytical edge, but a structural market risk that must be assessed before any F5 recommendation is activated.

**What the Oracle does not do with situational factors:**

Situational factors are supporting context within F5 evaluation. They may reinforce a thesis built on SP and lineup analysis. They are not sufficient, on their own, to constitute F5 edge. An F5 case built primarily on situational factors without a grounded SP matchup assessment does not meet the Handicap Quality standard.

**Common mistakes:**

- Applying full-game situational analysis identically to the F5 context without adjusting for the five-inning scope.
- Ignoring precipitation risk as an F5 Void factor when weather conditions create genuine early-game suspension risk.
- Overweighting scheduling factors that are primarily relevant to bullpen management and late-game strategy decisions outside the five-inning window.
- Treating park factors as an independent source of F5 edge rather than as a contextual modifier of the SP and lineup analysis.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Situational factors interact with all other categories but do not override them. Park factors amplify or reduce the expected scoring context established by the SP matchup and lineup analysis. Weather interacts specifically with lineup construction — fly-ball-heavy lineups are more affected by wind-out conditions in early innings than contact-oriented lineups. Precipitation risk is a standalone F5 pass consideration that does not strengthen or weaken other analytical categories.

---

### Section 6.6 — Market Context and Pricing

**Why it matters:**

The Oracle's F5 analytical position has no value as a recommendation unless the price at which the position can be entered offers expected value relative to the Oracle's probability estimate. The F5 market price encodes the market's assessment of the five-inning outcome probability. The Oracle must compare its five-inning probability estimate — derived from the categories above — to the market-implied probability and determine whether the available price justifies a recommendation.

F5 prices and full-game moneyline prices are not equivalent. The same team may be priced differently in the F5 and full-game markets because the market weights different factors for each scope. The Oracle's F5 probability estimate must be derived from F5-specific analysis, not transposed from a full-game probability estimate with a simple adjustment.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **F5 market-implied probability:** The probability the F5 odds imply for the recommended side, using standard odds-to-probability conversion including the vig. This is the market's stated five-inning assessment. The Oracle's F5 thesis must differ from it meaningfully.
- **Line movement context:** Has the F5 line moved since opening in a direction consistent or inconsistent with the Oracle's thesis? Line movement is relevant context, not a substitute for analysis.
- **Value threshold:** Is the available F5 price sufficient to justify the recommendation given the Oracle's analytical confidence and its five-inning probability assessment? The Oracle must be able to state explicitly why the F5 price is acceptable.
- **Price condition setting:** If the Oracle's analytical case depends on an F5 price not yet confirmed or at the margin of acceptability, a Conditional Play with an explicit price threshold is more appropriate than an unconditional play.
- **Comparison to the full-game moneyline:** If the F5 and full-game moneyline both appear to offer value on the same side, the Oracle must determine which market better expresses the thesis. Playing both markets simultaneously on the same directional thesis requires explicit justification beyond "both offer value."

**Common mistakes:**

- Transposing a full-game probability estimate directly to the F5 market without deriving the five-inning probability independently.
- Accepting an F5 price at face value because directional conviction is high, without explicitly comparing the price to the Oracle's five-inning probability estimate.
- Using F5 line movement as a substitute for F5 analysis rather than as supplementary context.
- Failing to recognize that F5 favorites are typically priced closer to even-money than full-game favorites because the five-inning window reduces the expected margin of superiority.
- Ignoring the vigorish when converting F5 odds to implied probability.

**Interaction with other categories:**

Market context is the final analytical filter after all other categories have been assessed. A strong F5 analytical case that does not translate into an acceptable price is a pass. Market context may also reveal that the F5 edge has been priced in through recent line movement, reducing or eliminating the Oracle's identified advantage before the recommendation is activated.

---

### Section 6.7 — Holistic Evidence Interpretation

After assessing each category individually, the Oracle must interpret the full body of F5 evidence together — not as a tally of favorable factors, but as a unified analytical picture of the first five innings.

In the F5 market, holistic interpretation carries a specific implication: the relative weight of analytical categories is shifted compared to the full-game moneyline. Starting pitcher quality, durability, and the platoon matchup within the five-inning window deserve greater analytical weight in the F5 holistic assessment. Bullpen considerations deserve substantially less weight unless specific early hook risk or opener deployment is present. Lineup analysis is filtered through the top-of-order lens that defines the F5 offensive context.

The Oracle must be explicit about which factors are carrying the most analytical weight in each F5 evaluation. A recommendation where the primary supporting evidence is bullpen quality, late-game scheduling effects, or a full-game offensive advantage that does not manifest in the first five innings is misaligned with the F5 market's analytical structure.

The Oracle must also calibrate its confidence honestly to the F5 window's inherent variance. A five-inning outcome is more volatile than a nine-inning outcome, and the Oracle's probability assessment must reflect that additional variance rather than presenting the five-inning picture with the same confidence it might express for a full-game thesis of equal apparent strength.

## Article VII — Evidence Hierarchy

### Section 7.1 — Why Evidence Quality Matters

Not all analytical inputs are equally reliable. The Oracle must distinguish between evidence that is confirmed and specific, evidence that is projected and plausible, and evidence that is assumed or narrative. This distinction is especially important in F5 evaluation, where the analytical case rests heavily on a single dominant variable — the starting pitcher matchup. Uncertainty in that variable, if not documented, can produce a recommendation that appears more supported than it is.

### Section 7.2 — Confirmed and Specific Evidence

The highest-quality evidence for an F5 recommendation is information that:

- Has been officially announced or confirmed before the analysis is documented
- Is specific to the game, matchup, or conditions being analyzed
- Can be verified by reference to a named source or data feed

Examples of confirmed, specific evidence in the F5 context:

- Both starting pitchers officially confirmed by both teams
- An officially announced lineup with a confirmed absence in the top of the order
- A published pitch count limit or innings restriction for a SP (a documented organizational protocol, not an assumed default)
- Confirmed meteorological data for an outdoor venue
- A documented opener deployment announced by the team

Confirmed evidence is the primary basis for F5 recommendations. An F5 recommendation that lacks confirmed SP information on one or both sides is at minimum a Conditional Play, not an unconditional recommendation.

### Section 7.3 — Projected and Plausible Evidence

Lower than confirmed evidence but still analytically usable is evidence that:

- Is not yet confirmed but is expected with reasonable basis (probable pitchers not formally announced, projected lineups not officially posted)
- Is drawn from established patterns documentably relevant to the current situation (a SP's well-documented TTO 2 performance profile)

Projected evidence must be flagged as projected. Where material to the F5 thesis — particularly when SP confirmation is projected rather than confirmed — a Conditional Play structure should be considered. An F5 recommendation that rests on a projected SP who is later scratched must produce a Void outcome.

### Section 7.4 — Narrative and General Evidence

The weakest category of evidence includes:

- Directional opinions unsupported by specific data points
- General narratives about team quality, recent performance, or pitching reputation
- Second-hand assessments without primary source documentation
- Any reasoning that relies on overall team strength rather than the specific five-inning window

In the F5 context, narrative evidence is susceptible to a specific error: applying full-game analytical narratives — this team has elite bullpen depth; this team's offense has been consistently strong all month — to a five-inning market where those narratives may have limited applicability. The Oracle must explicitly check whether the narrative evidence it is considering is genuinely material to the five-inning scope before treating it as supporting context.

### Section 7.5 — Evidence Conflicts

When F5 evidence points in different directions — a SP quality advantage offset by a pronounced platoon disadvantage against the opposing lineup, for example — the Oracle must address the conflict explicitly. The correct response to genuine analytical conflict is one of three:

1. Document both sides and explain why one factor outweighs the other in this specific five-inning context.
2. Classify the recommendation as a Conditional Play that waits for additional clarifying confirmation.
3. Pass, because the conflicting evidence indicates insufficient analytical clarity for an F5 recommendation.

### Section 7.6 — Documenting Uncertainty

Before first pitch, the Oracle must explicitly identify the major uncertainties remaining in its F5 picture — not just what it knows, but what it does not know and what it has estimated.

Three categories of information should be distinguished:

- **Confirmed:** Officially announced or verifiable from a primary source before the analysis is documented. Confirmed SP assignments, confirmed lineup absences, published pitch count limits, published weather data.
- **Estimated:** Expected or probable but not officially confirmed. Probable starters not formally announced, projected lineups not yet posted, expected weather based on forecasts, TTO performance profiles inferred from available data.
- **Unknown:** Material to the analysis but genuinely unavailable before first pitch. An undisclosed warm-up limitation for a SP, a lineup card not yet posted, a late-breaking weather update not yet issued.

Documenting uncertainty strengthens the F5 analytical record. A Conditional Play structure is often the correct response to material estimated or unknown information — particularly regarding SP confirmation — rather than an unconditional recommendation that implicitly treats estimated information as confirmed.

## Article VIII — Decision Framework

### Section 8.1 — Overview

The decision framework is the Oracle's structured process for moving from evidence assessment to an F5 verdict. It follows the Evaluation Lifecycle defined in the Oracle Evaluation Standard. The steps below describe how the Oracle approaches an F5 opportunity from first consideration to final verdict.

### Section 8.2 — Step 1: Identify the Candidate

A game enters F5 evaluation when the Oracle identifies a specific reason to assess it within the five-inning scope — typically a starting pitching matchup that creates an initial basis for a five-inning directional assessment. The Oracle does not evaluate every available F5 opportunity. A candidate must enter evaluation on the basis of a specific, articulable reason tied to the five-inning analytical window.

Valid reasons to identify an F5 candidate:

- A specific, documentable SP quality advantage in the five-inning context
- A confirmed lineup situation that creates a clear five-inning offensive picture
- An initial review suggesting a meaningful platoon or TTO matchup differential

Identifying a candidate is not a commitment. The Oracle has not committed to a play by deciding to assess an F5 opportunity.

### Section 8.3 — Step 2: Assess Each Analytical Category

The Oracle works through each of the Core Evaluation Categories in Article VI — Starting Pitcher Quality and Durability (6.1), Platoon Advantages and TTO (6.2), Bullpen and Early Hook Considerations (6.3), Early Lineup Strength (6.4), Situational and Environmental Factors (6.5), and Market Context and Pricing (6.6) — documenting what the evidence shows for each.

The assessment is written before the verdict is reached. The Oracle does not reach a conclusion and then build the F5 analysis backward. If a category is genuinely neutral or inapplicable in a specific game context, the Oracle documents why — it does not skip the category without acknowledgment.

### Section 8.4 — Step 3: Determine the Directional Thesis

After completing category assessment, the Oracle determines whether a clear five-inning directional thesis emerges. An F5 directional thesis is a specific, documentable claim that one side is more likely to be leading after five innings than the market implies, and that the basis for that claim is grounded in the five-inning analytical window — not the full-game outcome.

If no clear five-inning directional thesis emerges — if the evidence is balanced, conflicting, or insufficient — the outcome is a pass. The Oracle does not fabricate an F5 thesis from weak evidence, and it does not accept a full-game directional preference as a substitute for a five-inning thesis.

### Section 8.5 — Step 4: Evaluate the F5 Market Price

If a five-inning directional thesis exists, the Oracle evaluates the available F5 price. It estimates the probability implied by the current F5 odds — including the vig — and compares that to its own five-inning probability assessment. It determines explicitly whether the available price represents value, is neutral, or is unfavorable.

If the price is unfavorable relative to the F5 thesis, the outcome is a pass or a Conditional Play with an explicit price threshold.

### Section 8.6 — Step 5: Confirm F5 Market Appropriateness

The Oracle confirms that the F5 moneyline is the most appropriate market for its five-inning thesis. If the full-game moneyline, a game total, or the F5 run line is a more efficient expression of the analytical position, the Oracle either shifts to that market or passes on the F5 without a substitute recommendation. A substitute market requires its own evaluation under its applicable playbook.

### Section 8.7 — Step 6: Issue a Verdict

**Play:** The five-inning thesis is clear, evidence is sufficient across the relevant categories, all material pregame conditions are confirmed, the F5 market is appropriate, and the available F5 price offers value. The recommendation is activated.

**Conditional Play:** The five-inning thesis is clear, but one or more material factors — most commonly SP confirmation, lineup confirmation, or price threshold — have not yet been confirmed before the recommendation is completed. The Oracle documents specific conditions that must be met before activation. Unmet conditions before first pitch result in a Void outcome.

**Pass:** The evidence is insufficient, the five-inning thesis is unclear, the F5 price does not offer value, another market is more appropriate, or one or more pass conditions from Article X are triggered. Pass is always the correct outcome when qualifying evidence is absent.

## Article IX — Edge Identification Principles

### Section 9.1 — What Constitutes an Identifiable F5 Edge

An F5 edge exists when the Oracle's analytically derived probability for one side being ahead after five innings differs meaningfully from the probability implied by the F5 market price, and when that difference is based on specific, documentable evidence specific to the five-inning window.

The Oracle does not need certainty. It needs to be able to articulate specifically what the market has mispriced in the five-inning context and why. That articulation must be grounded in the categories defined in Article VI and must be independently derivable from the five-inning analytical scope.

### Section 9.2 — Common Sources of F5 Edge

The following are durable categories of analytical advantage that recur in F5 markets. This is not a checklist — not every edge type applies to every game.

**SP quality-differential edge:** One team's confirmed starting pitcher has a documentable quality advantage within the five-inning window that the F5 market has not fully priced. This is the most common and analytically durable source of F5 edge because the SP is the primary variable in the five-inning scope.

**Platoon-concentration edge:** The opposing lineup's handedness or pitch-preference composition creates a specific platoon advantage for the superior SP that is most concentrated in TTO 1 and TTO 2. F5 markets may price platoon advantages more slowly than full-game markets because the full-game market more commonly incorporates lineup depth and bullpen handedness management.

**TTO-profile edge:** One SP has a documented and specific pattern of maintaining effectiveness through TTO 2 while the opposing SP shows a documented TTO 2 decline. This creates a within-F5-window performance gradient that the market may not distinguish from a static SP quality comparison.

**Lineup-structure edge:** One team's top-of-order construction is specifically well-positioned to generate F5 offense against the opposing SP's profile, in a way that is stronger than the team's aggregate offensive metrics imply and that the F5 market has not distinguished.

**Information-based edge:** The market has not yet fully incorporated confirmed pregame information material to the five-inning outcome — a confirmed SP scratch, a confirmed top-of-order absence, a confirmed pitch count limit.

**Market-bias edge:** Public betting interest has moved the F5 price toward the more popular or narratively compelling side, creating value on the other side that the Oracle's independent five-inning analysis supports.

### Section 9.3 — Edge and Value Must Coexist

An F5 analytical edge and an acceptable F5 market price are both required for a recommendation. Neither alone is sufficient.

An F5 analytical edge exists when the Oracle's estimated five-inning probability for one side exceeds the probability the F5 market has priced into the available odds. A directional conviction — however strong — does not constitute an F5 recommendation if the available F5 price does not compensate for the probability being accepted.

This applies with particular force to F5 favorites. A team priced as a strong F5 favorite may carry low-value or negative-value odds even if the Oracle agrees with the directional assessment. The relevant question is not "does the Oracle believe this side is more likely to be ahead after five innings?" It is "does the available price on this side represent expected value relative to the Oracle's estimated five-inning probability?" If the answer is no, the outcome is a pass on the F5 market.

### Section 9.4 — What Is Not an F5 Edge

The following are common sources of misidentified F5 edge:

- **Bullpen quality advantage without early hook relevance:** A superior bullpen is not an F5 edge unless documented early hook risk or an opener deployment brings that bullpen into the five-inning window. Bullpen depth that matters only in the sixth inning and beyond has no F5 analytical weight.
- **Full-game offensive superiority:** A team that is superior over nine innings may not be proportionally superior over five innings if its offensive advantage is distributed through lineup spots that appear less frequently in TTO 1 and TTO 2.
- **Scheduling factors affecting the late game:** A team conserving its bullpen or managing its lineup for tomorrow's game is a full-game consideration. These decisions typically become consequential in innings outside the F5 window.
- **General team quality or recent winning performance:** The market prices general quality and recent performance efficiently. An F5 edge requires specific, documentable evidence about the five-inning window — not a directional opinion about which team is better overall.
- **Full-game line movement:** A line move on the full-game moneyline is relevant context for that market, not independent evidence of F5 edge. The Oracle derives its F5 probability estimate from F5-specific analysis.

## Article X — Pass Conditions

Each of the following conditions independently requires a pass on the F5 moneyline. A triggered pass condition requires a Pass regardless of how strong the case appears in other dimensions. This list addresses the conditions most material to the F5 market — it is not exhaustive.

### Section 10.1 — No Clear Five-Inning Directional Thesis

The Oracle cannot articulate a specific, documentable reason why one side is more likely to be leading after five innings than the F5 market implies. A general directional view about which team is better does not constitute a five-inning thesis. A full-game moneyline preference does not substitute for a five-inning analytical basis.

### Section 10.2 — Starting Pitcher Not Confirmed on the Thesis Side

The Oracle's F5 thesis depends on a specific starting pitcher who has not been officially confirmed and cannot reasonably be confirmed before first pitch. Unconfirmed SP status is the most common F5-specific pass trigger because SP quality is the market's primary analytical variable. An F5 thesis built on an unconfirmed starter cannot be unconditionally activated.

Where SP confirmation is expected before first pitch, a Conditional Play with an explicit confirmation condition may substitute for a pass. If SP confirmation is unlikely before first pitch, the recommendation is a pass.

### Section 10.3 — Price Does Not Compensate for Five-Inning Risk

The available F5 price on the recommended side does not offer sufficient expected value relative to the Oracle's five-inning probability estimate. A directional conviction, however strong, does not override a price that does not compensate for the probability being accepted in the five-inning window.

### Section 10.4 — Genuine Analytical Conflict

The five-inning evidence is materially conflicting across the analytical categories in a way that creates genuine uncertainty about the five-inning directional outcome — uncertainty that cannot be honestly resolved by weighing one factor against another. A pass acknowledges the honest state of the analysis.

### Section 10.5 — Another Market Is More Appropriate

The Oracle's thesis is more precisely or efficiently expressed in another market — the full-game moneyline, an F5 run line, or a game total. The Oracle evaluates the other market under its applicable playbook or passes without substitution.

### Section 10.6 — Significant Precipitation or Suspension Risk

The game's early weather conditions create a material risk that the game will not complete five innings, which would void the F5 bet under applicable market rules. This is an F5-specific pass condition: early suspension risk creates F5 Void exposure that does not exist in the same form for the full-game moneyline. The Oracle does not treat F5 Void risk as analytically neutral. If precipitation or suspension risk is material before first pitch, the F5 market is not the appropriate vehicle regardless of the directional thesis.

### Section 10.7 — Daily Volume Limit Would Be Exceeded

Activating the recommendation would exceed the daily play limit established by the Tracking Rules. Constitutional Rule 1 (No Forced Plays) and Tracking Rule 4 govern this condition. A strong F5 analytical case does not override a volume limit.

### Section 10.8 — Pregame Documentation Cannot Be Completed

The Oracle cannot complete all required pregame fields, all Core Evaluation Category assessments, and the Process Score before first pitch. Incomplete pregame documentation at first pitch is a failed-process record (Process Score: F). If the Oracle cannot document the F5 play properly before first pitch, it does not make the play.

## Article XI — Common Analytical Errors

The following errors represent the most frequent ways F5 analysis goes wrong. The Oracle must be able to recognize them in its own reasoning and correct them before a recommendation is made.

### Section 11.1 — Bullpen Contamination

Applying full-game bullpen depth analysis to an F5 evaluation without documented early hook risk or opener deployment. The F5 window ends before the bullpen's role becomes analytically dominant in most games. An F5 case that rests substantially on one team's bullpen superiority is analytically misaligned with the market. The Oracle does not bet full-game bullpen advantages in the F5 market unless those bullpen arms are specifically expected within the five-inning window.

### Section 11.2 — Full-Game Transposition

Carrying the full-game moneyline analysis directly into the F5 market without adjusting for the five-inning scope. The F5 is not a shortened moneyline. It is a market with a distinct analytical structure where different factors carry different weight. The Oracle derives its F5 analysis from the five-inning analytical categories, not by applying a discount factor to its full-game analysis.

### Section 11.3 — TTO Neglect

Ignoring Times Through the Order dynamics in the five-inning window. In a full-game analysis, TTO effects are spread across a broader sample. In the F5 context, TTO 2 performance in the fourth and fifth innings may be the analytically decisive factor. A SP with a pronounced TTO 2 decline carries elevated F5 late-window risk that must be assessed, not assumed away.

### Section 11.4 — Lineup Depth Confusion

Evaluating the full lineup's offensive quality without adjusting for the top-of-order concentration effect in the five-inning window. A team with strong full-game production driven primarily by its lower-order hitters may be weaker in the F5 context than its aggregate metrics suggest. The Oracle assesses which part of the lineup dominates the five-inning plate appearance distribution before drawing conclusions about offensive quality in this market.

### Section 11.5 — SP Confirmation Overconfidence

Treating a probable starter as a confirmed starter without explicitly acknowledging the analytical risk. The F5 market is more sensitive to SP uncertainty than the full-game moneyline because the SP is expected to cover nearly the entire F5 window. An SP scratch that produces a Void is a process risk the Oracle manages through Conditional Play structures where SP confirmation is material and outstanding.

### Section 11.6 — Precipitation Blindness

Failing to assess early-inning precipitation or suspension risk in outdoor venues before activating an F5 recommendation. F5 Void risk from weather suspension is an F5-specific market risk that requires explicit acknowledgment. The Oracle does not enter an F5 recommendation in a weather environment that creates material early-game suspension risk without addressing that void exposure as part of the decision.

### Section 11.7 — Price Insensitivity

Making an F5 directional call without explicitly comparing the F5 price to the Oracle's five-inning probability estimate. The F5 price and the full-game moneyline price are distinct market assessments. A directional case that has already been priced into the F5 odds does not constitute value. The Oracle compares its F5 probability estimate to the F5 market-implied probability — not to the full-game moneyline or to its general directional conviction.

### Section 11.8 — Forcing Action

Making an F5 recommendation because a preliminary look seemed interesting, or because the F5 appears to be a lower-commitment expression of a full-game thesis the Oracle is not fully confident in. Constitutional Rule 1 (No Forced Plays) applies to the F5 market with full force. The F5 market's partial-game scope does not lower the analytical threshold for a qualifying recommendation. A modestly interesting starting pitcher matchup without a clearly superior five-inning thesis is a pass, not an F5 play.

## Article XII — Relationship to the Evaluation Standard

### Section 12.1 — How This Playbook Serves the Five Evaluation Categories

The Oracle Evaluation Standard's five pregame evaluation categories apply to every Oracle recommendation, including all F5 moneyline plays. This playbook provides the F5-specific analytical content that informs those categories. The relationship is as follows:

---

**Game Selection** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.1)

The Oracle's decision to engage a specific game with an F5 recommendation is informed by the Core Evaluation Categories in Article VI. A game merits F5 evaluation when an initial review of Starting Pitcher Quality (6.1), Platoon and TTO dynamics (6.2), Lineup Strength (6.4), or Situational Factors (6.5) creates a specific, articulable five-inning basis for directional analysis. A game evaluated and found to lack that basis results in a pass — a correct Game Selection outcome.

---

**Market Selection** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.2)

The Oracle's decision to use the F5 moneyline rather than another market is governed by Article IV (When the F5 Market Is Appropriate) and Article V (When Another Market Is Preferable). A recommendation that uses the F5 market when the full-game moneyline, a game total, or the F5 run line better expressed the thesis is a Market Selection weakness.

---

**Handicap Quality** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.3)

The Oracle's analytical work — its assessment of SP quality and durability, platoon dynamics, TTO considerations, lineup structure, situational factors, and market context across the five-inning window — constitutes its F5 handicapping. Strong Handicap Quality for an F5 recommendation requires specific, confirmed SP information; honest engagement with TTO and platoon dynamics; documented top-of-order lineup analysis; and a clearly stated five-inning thesis grounded in the analytical categories above. Weak Handicap Quality results from vague SP analysis, skipped categories, narrative reasoning, or full-game analytical transposition applied without F5 adjustment.

---

**Value and Price** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.4)

The Oracle's assessment of the F5 market price, relative to its five-inning probability estimate, is governed by Article VI Section 6.6 (Market Context and Pricing) and Article VIII (Decision Framework). A recommendation that accepted an unfavorable F5 price without documentation, or that compared the F5 price to a full-game probability estimate rather than the Oracle's five-inning probability estimate, is a Value and Price weakness.

---

**Execution and Discipline** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.5)

The Oracle's compliance with Constitutional Rules, Tracking Rules, F5 pass conditions (Article X), and documentation requirements governs the Execution and Discipline category. An F5 recommendation that activates on an unconfirmed SP without a Conditional Play structure, enters a game with documented precipitation risk without acknowledging void exposure, fails to complete all pregame fields before first pitch, or violates the daily volume limit is an Execution and Discipline failure. An F Process Score is possible on this category alone if a Constitutional Rule is violated, regardless of the analytical quality of the recommendation.

### Section 12.2 — CLV for F5 Moneylines

Closing Line Value (CLV) for F5 moneyline bets is calculated by comparing the Oracle's F5 entry price to the closing F5 price on the same side. If the Oracle entered an F5 moneyline at +125 and the line closed at +105, the Oracle beat the F5 close. If the Oracle entered at +105 and the line closed at +125, the Oracle lost to the F5 close.

CLV for F5 moneylines does not require adjustment for spread movement. The F5 moneyline is a single price, not a spread-plus-price combination. The CLV comparison is made on the same F5 market — not against the full-game moneyline closing price.

At minimum, CLV tracking for F5 moneylines requires:
- The Oracle's F5 entry price recorded at the time of recommendation activation
- The closing F5 price on the same side, from the same market where available
- A directional comparison: whether the Oracle's entry price was more or less favorable than the closing price
- When a closing F5 price is unavailable — because the market was taken off the board or the data cannot be obtained — the closing price field is recorded as Unavailable per the Tracker Standard status markers; the entry price is still recorded

### Note on Future Evolution

This playbook establishes the Oracle's foundational F5 methodology. It represents the current analytical understanding of the five-inning market and the factors that most reliably govern it. As Oracle F5 evaluations accumulate and milestone audits generate validated findings, future editions may refine the analytical categories — particularly the evidence standards for early hook risk assessment, the treatment of hybrid opener-plus-bulk-pitcher deployments, and the calibration of TTO penalty weight relative to other factors within the five-inning scope.

The following represent possible areas for future analytical enhancement. Each requires validation through Oracle F5 evaluations and audit findings before any inclusion in this playbook:

- **Umpire tendencies:** Umpire strike zone profiles vary measurably and can affect pitch efficiency, plate discipline outcomes, and the overall pace of five-inning play. Whether umpire data constitutes a durable, confirmable source of F5 edge — and how it should be documented relative to the evidence tiers in Article VII — is an open question that Oracle evaluation may eventually answer.
- **Opener and bulk pitcher models:** As organizational opener usage evolves, a more formal methodology for evaluating opener-plus-bulk-pitcher F5 sequences may be warranted. The current framework addresses opener deployments as a modifier of the standard SP durability and sequence assessment; a dedicated evaluation model for this pitching structure may become appropriate as F5 opener evaluations accumulate.
- **First-time-through-the-order research:** The TTO framework in Section 6.2 is grounded in the well-documented performance differential between TTO 1, TTO 2, and TTO 3. Validated research specifically about the first-time-through-the-order advantage — how much a lineup underperforms its true quality on first exposure to a SP, and whether this effect is stable across pitcher types and lineup compositions — may refine the TTO analysis as Oracle F5 data accumulates.
- **Live lineup quality metrics:** The current playbook treats lineup quality and top-of-order construction as assessable from confirmed pregame lineup data. Analytical tools that measure the real-time quality of a lineup — accounting for platoon adjustments, individual performance variation, and other factors that shift game-to-game — may eventually offer a more precise F5 offensive assessment than static lineup analysis allows. Whether such metrics meet the evidence quality standards in Article VII is a question for future Oracle evaluation.

All amendments to this playbook are subject to the Constitutional Amendment Procedure defined in Oracle Constitution Article VI. No analytical observation, however compelling, modifies this playbook without Constitution Authority approval through that procedure. Evolution is continuous; it is also governed.
