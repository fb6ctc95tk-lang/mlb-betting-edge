## Preamble

The full-game total asks a question that no other market in this Oracle's universe asks: not which team wins, not who scores first, not who leads at the halfway point — but how many runs will be scored across the entire regulation game? The Over/Under market resolves on the combined run total for both teams at the end of regulation. It is, at its core, a bet on the game's run environment.

This is a fundamentally different analytical task from every other market this Oracle evaluates. Directional reasoning about team quality, pitching matchup dominance, or lineup superiority does not translate directly into a total runs assessment. A team with an excellent starting pitcher still contributes to a final run total through the middle innings, through the bullpen, through defensive play, and through the scoring by the other team's offense. A team with an explosive lineup still produces runs only when pitching allows baserunner accumulation. Total runs scored in a game emerge from the interaction of all these forces across all nine innings — not from one side's advantage over the other.

This playbook establishes the analytical methodology the Oracle uses when evaluating full-game Over and Under opportunities in Major League Baseball. It defines the run environment as the Oracle's core analytical concept, establishes the evaluation categories most relevant to total run production, and governs how the Oracle translates its run-environment estimate into a Play, Conditional Play, or Pass verdict.

This document is a methodology document. It teaches how the Oracle thinks about full-game run environment decisions. It does not contain current team information, current player data, seasonal statistics, historical trend databases, or sportsbook-specific guidance. The analytical principles that govern sound Totals evaluation are durable; specific data is not.

This playbook derives its authority from the Oracle Constitution (Level 1) and the Oracle Evaluation Standard (Level 2). It is consistent with and informed by the Oracle Audit Standard, Oracle Tracker Standard, and Oracle Playbook Standard. It may expand upon higher-level documents by adding market-specific detail, procedure, and analytical criteria. It may not contradict them.

## Article II — Scope

### Section 2.1 — Market Definition

This playbook governs the evaluation of full-game Over and Under markets in Major League Baseball.

**Over:** A bet that the combined run total for both teams exceeds the sportsbook's posted total at the end of regulation. The exact threshold and rounding rules vary by sportsbook.

**Under:** A bet that the combined run total for both teams falls short of the sportsbook's posted total at the end of regulation.

**Push:** When the combined run total equals the sportsbook's posted total exactly, the bet typically results in a push — the original stake is returned. The Oracle records this as a Push outcome consistent with the Tracker Standard.

These two outcomes — Over and Under — are the primary positions in a Totals market. Over and Under are opposing positions on the same run-environment probability, evaluated under the same analytical framework.

This playbook does not govern:

- Full-game moneyline bets
- Run line bets
- F5 (First Five Innings) totals
- First-inning NRFI/YRFI markets
- Team totals (one team's run total only)
- Player props or pitching props
- Alternate totals (which may differ in settlement structure)
- Any non-MLB market

### Section 2.2 — Regulation, Extra Innings, and Settlement Considerations

**Regulation play:** The standard Over/Under market resolves on the score at the end of nine innings — or the end of the last full inning in games where the home team leads after the top of the ninth and the bottom half is not played. The Oracle must verify whether the applicable sportsbook settles on nine full innings or on the official final score, as this distinction matters when the home team wins in walk-off fashion.

**Extra innings:** If the game requires extra innings — now played under special MLB rules that may change across seasons — the runs scored in extra innings count toward the final total in most sportsbook settlement structures. However, the Oracle must verify how the applicable book handles extra-inning play, as some books may post action and settle on extra-inning scoring while others structure their markets differently. The Oracle does not assume a universal extra-innings settlement rule.

**Suspended games:** A game suspended before reaching the official game threshold is typically declared no-action or void on the Totals market. The Oracle records this as a Void outcome. Where a suspended game is resumed and completed, the Over/Under market typically remains live and settles on the final combined score. Sportsbook rules on suspended games vary and must be verified.

**Rain-shortened games:** A game called due to weather after reaching the official game threshold (currently five innings in MLB) but before nine innings are complete is typically ruled an official game. Whether a rain-shortened official game settles the Over/Under on the truncated score or voids depends on the applicable sportsbook's house rules. The Oracle does not invent a universal rule; it verifies the relevant policy before any recommendation is activated.

**Settlement verification:** Material settlement uncertainty for a specific game scenario is a pass consideration under Section 10.7. Where the settlement structure for a specific scenario is unclear, the Oracle does not make an assumption — it treats the ambiguity as a material uncertainty.

### Section 2.3 — What the Full-Game Total Means Analytically

The full-game total encompasses every inning of a regulation game. Unlike the F5 market — which resolves on a single half-game pitching matchup — or NRFI/YRFI — which resolves on a single inning — the Over/Under market requires the Oracle to think about all nine innings:

- Two starting pitchers contributing for as many innings as they pitch
- Two bullpens contributing for all innings after each starter exits
- Both full lineups cycling through multiple times
- The cumulative effect of park, weather, umpire, and game-state dynamics across all nine innings

The result is that factors largely irrelevant in the NRFI/YRFI or F5 contexts become primary analytical obligations in the Totals market: bullpen quality and depth, full-lineup offensive output, fatigue and workload effects, managerial deployment patterns, and the aggregate environmental conditions that persist across all nine innings.

### Section 2.4 — What Makes This Market Structurally Different

The full-game Totals market is analytically distinct from every other market this Oracle evaluates. The distinction is not merely one of scope — it is structural.

**Different from the full-game moneyline:** The moneyline asks which team wins. It is a directional question: who is better? The Totals market is not directional. The better team winning a 2–1 game contributes less to the total than the weaker team winning a 9–7 game. The Oracle's Totals analysis does not produce a directional lean on which team is superior. It produces a run-environment estimate — the expected combined scoring from both teams together.

An excellent starting pitcher on one team does not automatically support an Under. That pitcher must be assessed not only for his own run-prevention quality but for how many innings he is expected to pitch, what his bullpen situation looks like after he exits, and whether his opponent's starting pitcher will also limit scoring or contribute to an early exit that opens the game to broader bullpen exposure.

**Different from the First Five (F5):** The F5 market concentrates on the first five innings, governed primarily by starting pitcher quality. The Totals market requires full-game run-environment assessment. After five innings, both starters may have exited — and the remaining scoring, which may constitute a substantial portion of a high-total game, is entirely driven by bullpen quality and depth. The Oracle's F5 analytical framework is insufficient for Totals because it systematically underweights bullpen contribution and full-lineup cycling.

**Different from NRFI/YRFI:** The NRFI/YRFI market asks whether any run scores in approximately six defensive outs. Total runs over a full game across all nine innings is an entirely different scope. Evidence calibrated to first-inning run-scoring probability — leadoff hitter profile, first-inning command specifically, pitcher warm-up consistency — is not the same evidence that governs a nine-inning run-environment estimate.

**The Oracle's Totals question is:** What is the most honest estimate of the total number of runs both teams will combine to score across this full regulation game, and does the available Over or Under price represent value relative to that estimate?

## Article III — Full-Game Totals Market Philosophy

### Section 3.1 — Totals Are Run-Environment Probability Markets

The Totals market does not ask which team is better. It asks how many runs will be scored. The Oracle's task is to estimate the game's run environment as accurately as the available evidence allows, compare that estimate to the run total implied by the market price, and identify whether a meaningful, documentable disagreement exists that supports a recommendation.

The Oracle is not predicting an exact final score. It is asserting that the true run environment for this game meaningfully differs from what the posted total implies — in a specific, evidence-grounded direction.

### Section 3.2 — Both Offenses and Both Defenses Contribute

A run-environment estimate requires assessing all four sides of the game: both offensive lineups and both pitching-plus-defense combinations. A game's total run output is not produced by one side — it is produced by the interaction of both offenses against both pitching staffs, in a shared park and weather environment, across all nine innings.

The Oracle does not evaluate the Over or Under based on one team's offensive profile or one team's pitching without assessing the corresponding counterparty. An explosive offense facing a dominant pitching staff may produce a low total despite the offense's quality. An elite starting pitcher may still allow a moderate number of runs when the opposing team's lineup works deep counts, extracts walks, and then faces a fatigued bullpen in the later innings. Isolation of any one factor produces an incomplete run-environment picture.

### Section 3.3 — Neither Offense Nor Pitching Should Be Analyzed in Isolation

Neither the pitching matchup nor the offensive lineups alone determines the expected run total. Both matter. They interact. A pitching-first analysis that stops when both starters look strong misses the critical question of what happens when those starters exit. An offense-first analysis that fails to account for the pitching matchup does not produce a calibrated run-environment estimate.

The Oracle explicitly requires both pitching and offensive analysis for every Totals evaluation, together with a synthesis that assesses their interaction — not a simple addition of favorable indicators from each side.

### Section 3.4 — Low-Scoring Games May Still Contain Excellent Offenses

An Under thesis does not require weak offenses. A game can have two elite offensive lineups and still project toward the Under if both starting pitchers are dominant, both bullpens are fresh and deep, and the park and weather conditions suppress run scoring. The Oracle does not treat offensive quality as automatically pointing toward the Over. Offensive quality must be evaluated in the context of the specific pitching and run-environment conditions it will face.

### Section 3.5 — High-Scoring Games May Still Feature Elite Starting Pitchers

An Over thesis does not require poor pitching. A game can have two quality starting pitchers and still project toward the Over if the bullpens are depleted, the park inflates run scoring significantly, the weather amplifies offense, or both lineups are historically effective at working deep into counts and extracting high pitch counts that force early starter exits. The Oracle does not treat elite pitching quality as automatically pointing toward the Under. Pitching quality must be evaluated for both its immediate run-prevention effect and its durability across the innings the pitcher is expected to cover.

### Section 3.6 — Pace of Scoring Is Less Important Than Expected Total Scoring

Whether runs score early or late in a game is not directly relevant to the Totals market — all runs count equally at the end of regulation regardless of when they scored. The Oracle does not weight first-inning or early-inning run probability more heavily when evaluating the full-game total. What matters is the cumulative expected run output across all nine innings — including starter, bullpen, and environmental contributions throughout the game.

### Section 3.7 — Price Must Always Be Evaluated Alongside Probability

An analytically sound run-environment estimate has no value as a recommendation unless the available Over or Under price offers expected value relative to that estimate. The Oracle's run-environment assessment must always be compared to the probability implied by the market price. A conviction about the run environment — however well-grounded — is not a recommendation if the available price does not compensate for the probability being accepted.

### Section 3.8 — The Oracle Estimates Expected Scoring, Not Exact Final Scores

The Oracle's Totals analysis produces a run-environment estimate — a directional assessment of whether total scoring is likely to be higher or lower than the posted total. It does not produce a predicted exact score. The run-environment estimate informs the Over or Under direction by identifying whether the market-implied total is meaningfully above or below the Oracle's honest probability-weighted scoring assessment. Apparent precision in a run total estimate — "I think this game produces 7.3 runs" — that is not grounded in the quality and completeness of the available evidence is false precision and is not an Oracle output.

## Article IV — When the Full-Game Totals Market Is Appropriate

The Over or Under market is the right vehicle when all of the following conditions hold:

### Section 4.1 — The Thesis Is Specifically About Full-Game Run Environment

The Oracle's analytical conviction is concentrated on the expected total run output for the full regulation game — not on which team wins, not on the first inning, and not on the five-inning pitching picture. A thesis that is fundamentally directional — about which team is better — or scope-limited — about how the first five innings unfold — belongs in a different market.

### Section 4.2 — Both Starting Pitchers and Lineup Constructions Are Assessable

The Oracle has sufficient evidence to assess both starting pitchers' quality, durability, and expected inning coverage, and both lineups' offensive run creation capability. A Totals recommendation that cannot adequately assess one side's primary pitching or offensive contributor is analytically incomplete.

### Section 4.3 — The Bullpen Environment Is Sufficiently Known

Because the Totals market requires post-starter innings assessment, the Oracle must have adequate information about both teams' bullpen situations: key arm availability, recent workload, and general deployment patterns. Significant bullpen uncertainty — particularly on one or both sides — is a pass consideration under Section 10.5.

### Section 4.4 — The Run Environment Is Confirmable

Material environmental factors — park dimensions, weather conditions, roof status where applicable — are either confirmed or confirmable before first pitch. Significant weather or environmental uncertainty is a pass consideration under Section 10.4.

### Section 4.5 — The Price Is Acceptable

The Over or Under price on the recommended side is consistent with the Oracle's estimated run-environment probability. The Oracle can articulate why the price is fair or favorable. See Article IX for the price evaluation framework.

## Article V — When Another Market Is Preferable

The Oracle must actively consider alternative markets before committing to a Totals recommendation.

### Section 5.1 — Prefer the Full-Game Moneyline When

- The Oracle's primary conviction is directional — about which team is better, more likely to win, or has a meaningful quality advantage — rather than about the total run environment.
- The Oracle cannot clearly identify whether the run environment is elevated or suppressed relative to the posted total, but can identify the directional leader in the matchup.

### Section 5.2 — Prefer the F5 Total or F5 Moneyline When

- The Oracle's primary conviction is specifically about the five-inning starting pitching matchup rather than the full-game run environment.
- The Oracle has strong confidence in the starting pitching picture but significant uncertainty about one or both bullpens, making the full-game total difficult to estimate with confidence.

### Section 5.3 — Prefer NRFI or YRFI When

- The Oracle's primary conviction is specifically about first-inning scoring probability — the opening inning pitcher-lineup matchup — rather than the nine-inning run environment.

### Section 5.4 — Pass When No Market Is Superior

If the Oracle cannot identify a market that efficiently expresses its analytical position at an acceptable price, the correct outcome is a pass. The absence of a cleanly expressible run-environment thesis is a pass condition, not an invitation to find the least-bad market.

## Article VI — Core Evaluation Categories

The following categories define the specific analytical factors the Oracle must assess when evaluating a full-game Over or Under opportunity. These categories provide the market-specific content that fills the Evaluation Standard's Handicap Quality and Game Selection categories for Totals decisions.

Each category must be explicitly addressed in the pregame documentation. The Oracle does not assume a category is neutral without assessing it. Because the Totals market spans the full regulation game, every category must be considered for both teams and for all nine innings — not only the first five or only the first inning.

---

### Section 6.1 — Starting Pitching

**Why it matters:**

Starting pitchers are the primary run-prevention mechanism for the first portion of the game and represent the most significant individual run-environment variable the Oracle can assess in advance. Their quality, durability, and efficiency determine not only how many runs they surrender while pitching but also how many innings they cover before the bullpen takes over. A starting pitcher who exits in the fourth inning creates a very different total run environment than one who completes seven strong innings.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Quality:** What is the pitcher's overall run-prevention quality? The Oracle uses evidence specific to the current season and recent performance, not historical reputation alone. Strikeout rate, walk rate, hard-contact rate, xERA, FIP, and similar skill-based metrics provide a more durable picture of true run-prevention quality than ERA alone.
- **Strikeouts:** High strikeout rates suppress the number of balls in play, reducing the probability that batted-ball events produce runs. A pitcher with elite strikeout ability limits the opportunities for runs to score through contact.
- **Walks:** High walk rates create baserunner accumulation risk that amplifies the probability of multi-run innings. Walk tendency is a primary skill indicator and a key variable in first-inning through full-start run-environment assessment.
- **Hard contact and home-run susceptibility:** A pitcher who allows significant hard contact — particularly fly-ball contact in a run-friendly park or weather environment — carries elevated risk of multi-run events. Home-run susceptibility is separately assessed because a single home run produces one or more runs immediately, and park and weather conditions amplify or suppress this risk.
- **Platoon matchups:** Starting pitchers have documented platoon splits. The Oracle assesses whether the opposing lineup's handedness composition creates a systematic platoon advantage or disadvantage for the pitcher across the full lineup exposure.
- **Expected innings (durability):** How deep into the game is this pitcher expected to go? A pitcher expected to cover only four or five innings because of pitch count management, a recent workload concern, or an organizational plan to deploy him in a limited role creates a larger bullpen window — and more run-environment uncertainty — than a durable workhorse expected to complete seven innings. Pitch count efficiency is the primary durability signal: pitchers who work efficiently in early counts cover more innings.
- **Pitch efficiency:** Pitchers who work deep counts, walk batters, and face prolonged at-bats accumulate pitch counts faster and are more likely to exit before the later innings. A high-pitch-count first two or three innings substantially reduces the probability of a long outing. The Oracle assesses whether each pitcher's efficiency profile is consistent with the expected innings coverage.

**Interaction with bullpen usage:**

Starting pitcher analysis is inseparable from bullpen analysis in the Totals context. A starter's expected innings directly determines when the bullpen takes over. A durable, efficient starter who covers seven or more innings dramatically limits bullpen exposure. A starter who exits after four innings due to pitch count, performance, or a planned hook creates a five-inning bullpen window that becomes a major Totals factor. The Oracle does not analyze starting pitching and bullpen independently — it assesses the handoff point and what the combined pitching picture looks like across all nine innings.

**What the Oracle avoids:**

Full-game ERA and win-loss records are the weakest starting-pitcher evidence. The Oracle relies on skill-based indicators grounded in the current season and recent performance. Reputation from prior seasons, particularly without current-year skill support, is treated as narrative evidence — the weakest tier.

**Common mistakes:**

- Treating one dominant starter as an automatic Under without assessing the opponent's pitching, both bullpens, and the run environment.
- Using historical season ERA from two or three seasons ago as a primary quality indicator.
- Failing to assess expected innings alongside pitching quality.
- Ignoring platoon effects on run-environment contribution.

### Section 6.2 — Bullpen Environment

**Why it matters:**

The full-game Totals market is the only market where bullpen quality is a primary analytical category rather than a secondary or largely irrelevant one. In a nine-inning game, bullpen pitchers typically cover three to five innings — often more in high-pitch-count or early-exit scenarios. The runs scored after the starting pitcher exits can equal or exceed the runs scored while the starter is pitching. Ignoring the bullpen in a Totals analysis is a structural error.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Bullpen quality:** What is the overall quality level of each team's bullpen available for this game? The Oracle assesses run-prevention quality at the bullpen level, not merely by identifying one or two key relievers. A team whose entire bullpen is mediocre contributes a meaningfully different run-environment picture than a team with two elite late-inning arms and serviceable depth.
- **Leverage arms and high-leverage availability:** Are the highest-quality relievers — the closers, setup men, and elite middle-inning arms — available for this game? A team whose primary leverage arm is unavailable due to prior-night usage, injury, or rest presents a weaker bullpen picture than one with a full complement available.
- **Recent workload and fatigue:** Have key bullpen arms been heavily used in the preceding days? Bullpen fatigue is a legitimate and documentable run-environment factor. A bullpen that has pitched significant innings across the previous two or three days may be less effective, with lower-quality options or fatigued arms being deployed in leverage situations.
- **Expected availability:** Some bullpen arms may be unavailable due to injury, roster status, or documented rest-day management. The Oracle assesses which arms are genuinely available for this game — not the full theoretical bullpen depth on the roster.
- **Managerial deployment patterns:** How does this manager deploy the bullpen? Some managers protect high-leverage arms for specific situations and allow games to evolve to avoid burning them. Others are more willing to use their best available arms in earlier leverage situations. Deployment patterns affect when and how frequently the best arms appear and therefore affect the expected bullpen run-environment contribution.
- **Bullpen depth:** If the high-leverage arms are unavailable or exhausted, what quality of pitching remains? A bullpen with shallow depth behind its top options carries higher run-environment risk in games where those top options have been used.
- **Handedness balance:** Does the bullpen have adequate left-handed relief options to manage platoon matchups in the later innings? A one-dimensional bullpen may create specific vulnerabilities against certain lineup constructions in the sixth through ninth innings.

**The starter-to-bullpen transition:**

The handoff from starting pitcher to bullpen is the most significant structural event in a full-game Totals evaluation. The timing and nature of this transition determines how many innings each tier of pitching covers and therefore directly shapes the nine-inning run-environment picture.

- **Expected starter exit:** The Oracle's bullpen analysis begins with an estimate of when each starting pitcher is expected to exit. A pitcher expected to complete seven innings leaves a two-inning bullpen window. A pitcher operating under a clear pitch count plan who is unlikely to complete five innings leaves a four-to-five-inning window. The expected exit point is determined by workload context, recent pitch count efficiency, known deployment plans, and the game's scoring development. A starter who falls behind early may exit sooner than his normal exit point, expanding the bullpen window beyond what pregame analysis anticipated.
- **Leverage reliever usage:** Not all bullpen innings are created equal. High-leverage situations — close games in late innings — bring the team's best available arms. Blowout situations may see lower-quality depth arms. The Oracle assesses what the expected game state will be at the handoff point and which leverage tier of the bullpen is likely to be deployed in the innings that follow.
- **Bullpen sequencing:** Managers typically deploy their bullpen in a structured sequence: lower-leverage middle-relief innings early in the bullpen window, followed by higher-leverage setup and closing arms in the final innings. When key arms are unavailable, this sequencing is disrupted — lower-quality arms may appear in higher-leverage situations, amplifying run-scoring risk. The Oracle assesses not just total bullpen quality but the likely sequencing given arm availability and recent deployment.
- **Timing uncertainty:** The exact inning at which the starter exits is not knowable before first pitch. A starter who struggles in the fourth inning exits earlier and creates a longer bullpen window; one who cruises through seven exits later and leaves a shorter window. This uncertainty is a structural feature of the Totals market. The Oracle accounts for it by assessing the range of plausible exit scenarios rather than assuming the starter pitches exactly to the expected exit point. High command variability, a difficult early-inning matchup, or a known pitch count ceiling all create timing uncertainty that widens the bullpen analysis.

Why this matters for Totals: the run-environment during the bullpen phase is often materially different from the run-environment during the starting phase. A game with two excellent starters and two depleted bullpens may have low expected scoring in innings 1–5 and elevated expected scoring in innings 6–9, producing a net game environment that is different from what a starting-pitcher-only analysis would suggest. The transition point is where that shift occurs, and its timing is uncertain. The Oracle requires both the starting pitching assessment and the bullpen assessment to be integrated through an honest analysis of the expected handoff structure.

**Bullpen uncertainty:**

Bullpen situations can change between the time of analysis and first pitch — a reliever may be added to the injured list, placed on rest, or moved to unavailability. The Oracle accounts for bullpen uncertainty by assessing what is confirmed versus what is projected, and by noting where significant bullpen changes before first pitch could materially affect the recommendation.

**Common mistakes:**

- Treating the Over/Under primarily as a starting pitching bet and neglecting bullpen analysis.
- Evaluating only the top one or two bullpen arms without assessing depth.
- Ignoring documented bullpen fatigue from prior-day usage.
- Treating a team's full bullpen roster as equivalent to its available bullpen for this specific game.
- Failing to assess managerial deployment tendencies when they are documentable.

### Section 6.3 — Offensive Run Creation

**Why it matters:**

Runs must be scored for the Over to win. The Oracle's run-environment estimate requires a calibrated assessment of each team's full offensive run creation capability — not just the top of the order, and not just against the starting pitcher. Full lineups cycle through multiple times in a nine-inning game, and the sixth, seventh, and eighth hitters contribute more run production in a full-game context than they do in a first-inning or five-inning frame.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Lineup quality and depth:** In contrast to the NRFI/YRFI analysis where the Oracle focuses almost entirely on the top three hitters, full-game Totals analysis requires assessment of the complete lineup. Runs scored in the fifth through ninth innings frequently involve the middle and bottom of the order. A lineup with significant depth — quality throughout positions 1–9 — contributes more to the run environment than a lineup with a dangerous top of the order and a weak bottom half.
- **Contact profile:** Lineups with high contact rates make more balls in play, generating more opportunities for runs to score through hit-chain sequences. High-contact lineups also extend innings and drive up pitch counts, increasing the probability of reaching the bullpen sooner.
- **Power and extra-base-hit production:** Power in the lineup generates runs more efficiently — home runs score baserunners without requiring hit chains, and extra-base hits advance runners into scoring position with fewer outs required. Power-heavy lineups elevate Over probability, particularly in run-friendly parks or weather conditions.
- **Walk generation:** Walk production creates baserunners without requiring contact, accumulates pitch counts, and amplifies the value of subsequent power or contact events. Lineups with high walk rates are more likely to produce multi-run innings by getting runners on base consistently.
- **Strikeout profile:** High-strikeout lineups produce fewer balls in play, make outs more quickly, and allow pitchers to work more efficiently. This directly suppresses scoring and represents an Under-supporting offensive characteristic.
- **Platoon advantage:** Does the lineup have a systematic handedness advantage against the opposing starting pitcher? A lineup stacked with same-handed hitters against a pitcher with significant platoon splits will see that advantage amplified when the pitcher with opposite-handed relievers is brought in. The Oracle assesses platoon exposure across the full lineup, not only the top of the order.
- **Baserunning:** Aggressive baserunning can convert singles to scoring opportunities and manufacture runs without requiring extra-base hits. While this is a secondary factor in most Totals evaluations, teams with documented aggressive baserunning tendencies may be slightly more efficient in converting baserunner situations into runs.
- **Offensive consistency and clustering:** Does this lineup score in multi-run innings or predominantly in one-run dribbles? Offense that clusters — that produces multiple-run innings rather than stringing isolated singles and walks that end up stranding runners — is more efficient at converting scoring opportunities into runs. The Oracle considers whether the lineup's offensive profile suggests clustered or distributed run production.
- **Recent lineup availability:** Are the primary offensive contributors healthy and in the lineup? A key hitter absence — particularly a middle-of-the-order power bat — may materially affect the lineup's run-creation profile. Confirmed lineup information is required before activating any unconditional recommendation.

**Complete lineup emphasis:**

The Totals market is the Oracle market that most requires full-lineup analysis. The Oracle does not apply top-of-the-order thinking to full-game offensive assessment. Run production in the sixth, seventh, and eighth innings frequently involves hitters who bat fifth through eighth. A comprehensive run-environment estimate accounts for the quality of the full batting order.

**Common mistakes:**

- Applying top-of-the-order analysis from NRFI/YRFI or F5 frameworks to a full-game Totals evaluation.
- Treating team OPS or other aggregate offensive metrics as equivalent to run-environment contribution without assessing lineup depth and construction.
- Ignoring confirmed late-lineup hitter absences that reduce full-game offensive depth.
- Treating a team's full-season offensive statistics as an adequate substitute for recent performance assessment.

### Section 6.4 — Offensive Versus Pitching Interaction

**Why it matters:**

Assessing the offensive run-creation capability and the pitching run-prevention quality of each team independently produces two partial pictures. The run environment for each half of the game — each team's offense against the opposing team's pitching — emerges from their specific interaction. The Oracle requires a holistic interaction assessment that moves beyond the simplistic framing of "good offense versus bad pitcher" and identifies the specific factors that modulate how well the offense will perform against this specific pitching in this specific context.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Pitch-type matchups:** Does the opposing lineup have a documented strength or weakness against the specific types of pitches the starter relies upon? A lineup that struggles against breaking balls facing a starter whose primary weapon is a sharp curveball represents a specific run-suppression interaction. A lineup with excellent fastball recognition facing a fastball-heavy starter represents an elevated run-creation interaction.
- **Handedness dynamics:** Left-handed lineup-heavy teams against left-handed starters, or right-handed lineups against right-handed starters, produce platoon matchup dynamics that may amplify or suppress scoring relative to neutral expectations. The Oracle assesses the full lineup's handedness composition against each pitcher's platoon profile.
- **Strikeout vs. contact interaction:** A high-strikeout pitcher facing a high-contact lineup may suppress the lineup's contact advantage. A contact pitcher facing a high-strikeout lineup may find his contact-inducing approach amplified by the lineup's tendency to make outs via strikeout regardless of approach. The interaction of the pitcher's strikeout profile and the lineup's contact or strikeout profile is a run-environment modulator.
- **Walk pressure dynamics:** A pitcher with a walk tendency facing a patient lineup that draws walks compounds the run-environment risk substantially. A pitcher with excellent command facing an aggressive lineup that rarely draws walks reduces the baserunner accumulation probability. The Oracle assesses the walk-pressure interaction for each half of the game.
- **Power versus fly-ball profile:** A pitcher who induces fly balls facing a lineup with significant power threat — particularly in a fly-ball-friendly park or with favorable wind — creates an elevated Over risk through the home-run pathway. A ground-ball pitcher facing a power-heavy lineup significantly reduces the home-run pathway even in a run-friendly park.
- **Ground-ball interaction:** Ground-ball pitchers against high-groundball-rate lineups produce more outs without the home-run risk but may still allow hit-chain runs. Ground-ball pitchers against high-fly-ball lineups create a contact-type mismatch that typically favors the pitcher. The Oracle identifies which contact type interaction is likely to dominate.

**What the Oracle avoids:**

The Oracle does not reduce the interaction analysis to a directional "good offense / bad pitcher" or "bad offense / good pitcher" framing. Run environments emerge from complex interactions, and the Oracle must engage with those interactions specifically. A strong offense facing a weak pitcher does not automatically produce a high run total if the park suppresses scoring, the weather is cold and calm, and both bullpens are fresh. The specific factors that determine how this offense performs against this pitching in these conditions are what the Oracle must assess.

**Common mistakes:**

- Treating the interaction analysis as a competition that one side "wins" rather than as a specific modulation of run-creation probability.
- Ignoring pitch-type evidence and reducing the interaction to ERA vs. OPS comparisons.
- Failing to assess both halves of the game — the home team's offense against the visiting pitcher and the visiting team's offense against the home pitcher — with equal analytical rigor.

### Section 6.5 — Run Environment Factors

**Why it matters:**

The physical context of the game — ballpark, altitude, weather, and playing conditions — is a systematic modifier of expected run scoring that affects both offenses equally and independently of team quality. Two otherwise equivalent matchups played at different ballparks or in different weather conditions may have meaningfully different expected run totals. The Oracle treats run environment factors as a common input that modifies the baseline run expectation produced by the pitching and offensive analysis.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Park factor:** Some ballparks systematically inflate run scoring; others suppress it. The Oracle accounts for park factor as a run-environment baseline modifier. A game played in a historically high-run-environment park shifts the expected total upward; a game in a suppressive park shifts it downward. Park factors should be applied contextually rather than mechanically — they interact with specific weather conditions, wind directions, and the specific offensive and pitching profiles of the teams involved.
- **Altitude:** Ballparks at higher altitudes reduce air resistance on batted balls, increasing fly-ball carry and home-run probability. This effect is systematic and well-documented. The Oracle accounts for altitude when the game is played at a venue where altitude meaningfully affects ball flight.
- **Park dimensions:** Beyond park factor as an aggregate, the Oracle considers specific dimension-profile interactions: a short right-field porch interacts differently with a left-handed pull-power lineup than with a contact-oriented lineup; a cavernous center field suppresses triples and deep fly-ball outs that would otherwise reach the fence in a shorter park.
- **Wind direction and strength:** Wind blowing out toward the outfield amplifies fly-ball carry and home-run probability. Wind blowing in suppresses fly-ball distance and reduces run scoring through the power pathway. Strong crosswinds affect both pitchers' control and batters' timing. The Oracle assesses the confirmed wind direction and speed for outdoor venues and evaluates its interaction with the specific fly-ball tendencies, power profiles, and pitching styles involved.
- **Temperature:** Cold temperatures suppress offensive output by reducing bat-head speed and exit velocity, reducing batted-ball distance, and affecting pitcher grip and command. Extreme heat can affect pitcher effectiveness and bullpen depth through fatigue. Temperature effects are most material at the extremes and interact specifically with the pitching and offensive profiles of the teams involved.
- **Humidity:** High humidity can slightly suppress ball flight in some conditions and may affect pitcher grip. While less material than temperature or wind in most cases, humidity is a secondary run-environment consideration in extreme cases.
- **Roof status:** For venues with retractable roofs, whether the roof is open or closed determines the entire weather environment for the game. Confirmed roof-closed status eliminates wind, temperature, and precipitation as active factors. Confirmed roof-open status exposes the game to the outdoor conditions. Roof status must be confirmed before any recommendation at a retractable-roof venue is activated.
- **Visibility:** Day games with strong sun or poor visibility conditions can affect a batter's ability to pick up the ball out of the pitcher's hand, suppressing offensive production. The Oracle considers visibility conditions when they are documentable and material.
- **Playing surface:** The surface type — natural grass versus artificial turf — affects batted ball speed through the infield, groundball hit rates, and some pitcher tendencies. While a secondary factor in most evaluations, turf parks may modestly amplify offense on groundball singles that get through the infield faster. The Oracle considers surface type as a minor run-environment modifier where evidence supports it.

**What the Oracle does not do with run environment factors:**

Run environment factors are modifiers applied to a grounded analytical assessment — they do not create scoring by themselves and do not substitute for pitching or offensive analysis. A strong wind blowing out is not a standalone Over argument unless the specific pitching and offensive profiles of the teams involved interact meaningfully with that condition. The Oracle does not produce environmental narratives that substitute for fundamental analysis.

Run environment factors affect both teams' scoring simultaneously. A park that inflates run scoring does so for both offenses. The Oracle does not apply park factor as a one-sided advantage.

**Double-counting risk:**

Environmental factors are shared between both halves of the game. Wind direction, temperature, roof status, and altitude apply equally to both offenses and both pitching staffs. The Oracle applies these as common modifiers to the overall run-environment picture — not as separate supporting inputs for each team's offensive or pitching analysis. Counting a shared environmental factor twice — once in the assessment of Team A's offense and again in the assessment of Team B's offense — inflates the apparent run-environment case. See also Section 6.9 on holistic interpretation.

### Section 6.6 — Umpire Context

**Why it matters:**

The home plate umpire's strike zone tendencies can materially affect walk frequency, strikeout rates, and overall scoring pace across a full nine-inning game. A generous zone reduces walk rates and amplifies pitcher efficiency; a tight zone increases walk rates, leads to more deep counts, and drives up pitch counts — accelerating starter exits and creating more bullpen exposure. In the Totals context, umpire effects operate across all nine innings rather than just the first inning, making their cumulative impact potentially more significant than in narrower markets.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Strike zone tendencies:** Where umpire zone data is confirmable, the Oracle assesses whether the assigned umpire has a documented tendency toward a wide or tight zone. A historically tight umpire increases walk probability, which may amplify run-scoring and push the total toward the Over. A historically generous umpire increases strikeout probability and pitcher efficiency, which may suppress run-scoring and push the total toward the Under.
- **Walk and strikeout environment:** The Oracle assesses the umpire's documented effect on walk rates and strikeout rates in prior games and evaluates how that tendency interacts with the command profiles and walk tendencies of both starting pitchers in this game.
- **Consistency:** A consistent umpire — whether the zone is wide or tight — allows pitchers to pattern-match and adjust. An inconsistent umpire increases uncertainty for both pitchers and may elevate walk rates by making borderline pitches less predictable.

**What the Oracle avoids:**

Umpire analysis is supporting evidence in the Totals context, not primary evidence. A standalone umpire argument — "this umpire has a tight zone, therefore the Over" — without grounding in the pitching command analysis is insufficient. The umpire modifies the run environment; it does not independently create it. Umpire assignment confirmation must precede any recommendation for which umpire analysis is material.

**Common mistakes:**

- Building a primary Over or Under thesis on umpire tendencies without a stronger pitching-offensive interaction foundation.
- Treating projected umpire assignments as confirmed before official confirmation is available.
- Using umpire zone data from small or stale samples as if they were reliable predictors.

### Section 6.7 — Game Context

**Why it matters:**

Game scheduling, travel, rest, and situational factors can affect team preparation, lineup freshness, and pitching availability in ways that modulate the expected run environment. These factors are secondary and must be grounded in specific, documentable evidence — not in general narratives about fatigue or motivation.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Travel and time zone effects:** Teams playing on the road after crossing multiple time zones — particularly early in a travel series — may experience fatigue effects that modestly affect performance. The Oracle treats this as a minor supporting factor when the travel burden is documentable and extreme, not as a general narrative.
- **Rest days and scheduling:** A team coming off a day off may have a more available bullpen and better-rested position players. A team in the middle of a long homestand or road trip stretch may show fatigue effects in its pitching availability or lineup motivation.
- **Getaway games:** Teams with a daytime travel obligation immediately following an evening game — particularly in a getaway game situation — may have rest-limited lineup options and a manager more focused on pitcher preservation. Getaway game tendencies, where documentable, may affect how aggressively managers use their bullpen.
- **Doubleheader effects:** In scheduled doubleheaders, bullpen availability may be significantly affected by the split or back-to-back game structure. The Oracle accounts for the impact on bullpen depth when the game is the first or second game of a doubleheader.
- **Lineup rotation:** Teams managing a long schedule may rotate starting players or manage key hitters' workloads. Where lineup rotation is confirmed or expected, the Oracle treats reduced lineup quality as a run-environment modifier.
- **Motivation when evidence exists:** The Oracle does not assume motivational effects without specific, documentable evidence. A team eliminated from playoff contention facing a meaningless late-season game may show lineup management effects that are documentable. The Oracle does not speculate about motivation without specific, verifiable behavioral evidence.

**What the Oracle avoids:**

Game context factors must be grounded in documentable, specific evidence. General narratives about travel fatigue, team morale, or motivational state without supporting behavioral or scheduling evidence are not analytical inputs. The Oracle does not build a Totals recommendation on game context factors alone, and does not treat game context as a primary analytical category.

### Section 6.8 — Market Context and Pricing

**Why it matters:**

An analytically sound run-environment estimate has no value as a recommendation unless the available Over or Under price offers expected value relative to that estimate. The posted total and associated juice encode the sportsbook's assessment of the expected run environment, net of hold. The Oracle must compare its run-environment estimate to the market-implied probability and determine whether the available price justifies a recommendation.

**What the Oracle must assess:**

- **Market-implied probability:** Convert the available Over or Under price to an implied probability using standard odds conversion, accounting for the vig. The implied probability reflects the sportsbook's stated run-environment assessment. The Oracle's estimate must differ from it meaningfully and in a documentable direction.
- **The posted total as implied probability:** The posted total also encodes the run-environment estimate as a whole number or half-number line. The Oracle assesses whether the posted number is meaningfully higher or lower than its own run-environment estimate — and whether the directional edge is sufficient to recommend the Over or Under given the associated price.
- **Alternate totals:** Where alternate total lines are available at different prices, the Oracle assesses whether a different total-line number offers better expected value for the same directional thesis. An alternate total that moves the line by half a run may significantly affect the probability structure of the bet.
- **Hold and vig:** In standard Over/Under markets, the combined implied probabilities exceed 100%, reflecting the sportsbook's hold. The Oracle accounts for the hold when assessing whether the available price offers genuine value.
- **Market movement:** Has the total moved significantly from its opening line? Sharp line movement — particularly movement that adjusts the total number itself rather than just the juice — may indicate that new information has entered the market. The Oracle assesses whether the market has already priced in the factors that underlie its thesis, potentially reducing or eliminating the available edge.
- **Price sensitivity:** The Over or Under price may be sensitive to late-breaking news — lineup changes, starting pitcher updates, weather confirmations. A recommendation that was valid at the opening price may not be valid if the line has moved materially before activation.
- **CLV baseline:** The CLV comparison for the Over must use the closing Over price on the same total market. The CLV comparison for the Under must use the closing Under price. Moneyline and F5 prices are not valid CLV baselines for full-game Totals comparisons.

**Push zone caution:**

When the Oracle's run-environment estimate falls close to the posted total — within the range where a small difference in actual scoring could produce a Push rather than a win — the Oracle applies additional caution before activating a recommendation.

A game expected to produce 8.2 combined runs against a posted total of 8 represents a small estimated edge toward the Over. Whether that edge is sufficient to justify a recommendation depends on the confidence of the estimate, the run variance of the game, and the available price. Small estimated edges near the posted total are particularly sensitive to:

- **Estimation uncertainty:** An assessment that the expected total is modestly above or below the posted line may be within the margin of error of the run-environment estimate itself. A small directional lean that cannot be articulated beyond "the expected total is modestly above the line" is not a sufficient basis for a recommendation.
- **Run variance:** In a high-variance game, the distribution of actual outcomes is wide. A small expected edge can easily be overcome by natural run-scoring variance. The closer the estimated total is to the posted number, the more a high-variance game environment undermines the confidence of the recommendation.
- **Push probability:** Games that produce exactly the posted total result in a push — the stake is returned but no profit is generated. When the expected run environment is close to the posted line, the probability of a push is meaningful and reduces the practical expected value of the bet.

The Oracle's standard for recommending the Over or Under near the posted total is more demanding than for games where the run environment clearly departs from the line. A small estimated edge close to the posted total — one without strong supporting evidence across multiple categories, or one in a high-variance game — should be treated as a Pass rather than a forced recommendation. The closer the estimated run environment is to the posted total, the stronger the analytical evidence and price must be to justify a Play verdict.

**Common mistakes:**

- Accepting an Over or Under price because directional conviction is high, without explicitly comparing the price to the Oracle's run-environment probability estimate.
- Failing to consider alternate totals when the primary line is at the margin of value.
- Using full-game moneyline movement as a proxy for Totals market validity.
- Setting a CLV baseline using a different market type.

### Section 6.9 — Holistic Run Environment Synthesis

**Why it matters:**

Run scoring in a baseball game emerges from the interaction of all contributing factors across all nine innings. The Oracle's run-environment estimate is not the arithmetic sum of the contributions from each evaluation category assessed independently. It is a synthesis — an honest integration of pitching quality, bullpen environment, offensive run creation, their specific interactions, the environmental modifiers, and the market context — into a single, directional run-environment assessment.

**What complete holistic synthesis includes:**

- The combined pitching run-prevention picture across all nine innings: starting pitchers' expected contributions plus each bullpen's expected coverage
- The combined offensive run-creation picture for both lineups across the full game
- The specific interaction effects between the offenses and pitching matchups — where the evidence identifies amplifying or suppressing factors
- The run-environment modifiers: park, weather, altitude, roof, and surface
- The umpire context where it is material and confirmed
- The game context factors where they are specific and documentable
- The market price and implied probability
- The primary risks to the recommendation — the most likely ways the thesis is wrong

**What complete holistic synthesis avoids:**

- Counting favorable indicators as votes: the Oracle does not recommend an Over because six categories appear to lean Over and three lean Under. The strength, quality, and materiality of the evidence in each category matter. A single highly material factor — a dominant pitching performance expected to extend six innings, combined with a depleted opposing bullpen — may outweigh four modestly unfavorable factors.
- Counting shared factors twice: park, weather, roof status, altitude, and umpire zone tendencies affect both teams' halves of the game simultaneously. These shared environmental factors are applied once to the combined run-environment picture — not once per team, which would artificially inflate the run-environment case in either direction.
- False precision: the Oracle does not attempt to produce a specific predicted run total stated with artificial precision. The run-environment assessment is directional — it establishes whether the expected total is meaningfully above or below the posted line — while being honest about the uncertainty inherent in the estimate.
- Isolated category dominance: a single category — even the most important one — does not determine the verdict in isolation. Starting pitcher quality must be combined with bullpen assessment, offensive analysis, environmental modifiers, and market context to produce a complete run-environment estimate.

**Scoring distribution and run timing:**

Games with identical expected run totals may have materially different scoring distributions. The Oracle estimates the overall run environment rather than predicting the exact timing or pattern of runs — but understanding distributional patterns informs where run-environment uncertainty is concentrated and sharpens the holistic synthesis.

Consider two games each expected to produce eight combined runs. In the first, runs are distributed steadily across many innings through consistent offensive traffic and moderate pitching quality. In the second, the same expected total comes from a three-run third inning when both starters lose command, followed by a five-run late surge when both bullpens are deployed under high leverage. Both games produce the same expected total. The distribution differs materially. The Oracle's recommendation — Over or Under on the posted line — is the same in both cases; what changes is the confidence and the nature of the risk.

Distributional patterns the Oracle recognizes:

- **Steady scoring:** Runs distributed across many innings through consistent offensive threat and moderate pitching from both staffs. Neither side dominates; scoring accumulates incrementally.
- **Concentrated scoring:** Most runs accumulate in one or two innings of breakout activity — a pitcher loses command in a specific frame, or a high-leverage bullpen moment coincides with the top of a powerful lineup. Total scoring may be unremarkable but arrives in bursts.
- **Early scoring:** Scoring concentrated in the first three innings, driven by first-time-through-the-order dynamics and starters who struggle early before settling in, or who face aggressive early-count lineups. The remaining innings may be quieter relative to the opening.
- **Late scoring:** Scoring concentrated in the later innings, typically bullpen-driven, when fatigued or lower-quality relievers replace durable starters. The game opens up after a quiet early phase.
- **Bullpen-driven scoring:** The majority of runs emerge after the starters exit. Common when both starters are effective through their expected innings but the bullpen phase produces a materially weaker run-prevention environment.
- **Starter-driven scoring:** The majority of runs are scored while the starters are pitching. Associated with early-inning command struggles or poor matchups for one or both starters before the bullpen stabilizes.

The Oracle does not need to predict which pattern will occur. Recognizing distributional tendencies helps identify where the run-environment variability is concentrated — whether the uncertainty is in the starting phase, the bullpen phase, or spread across both — which informs how confidently the holistic estimate supports an Over or Under recommendation.

**Expected runs and run variance:**

A run-environment estimate produces a directional assessment of total expected scoring. But two games with the same expected run total may carry very different levels of uncertainty around that estimate. The Oracle recognizes the distinction between expected runs and run variance — and accounts for variance when assessing the confidence of a recommendation, particularly near the posted total.

**Expected runs** represents the Oracle's best estimate of total scoring: the central tendency of the scoring distribution for this specific game given the available evidence. It is the directional signal that drives the Over or Under recommendation.

**Run variance** represents the width of uncertainty around that estimate — how far actual run totals might realistically deviate from the expected level. High-variance games carry more uncertainty about the outcome; low-variance games cluster more tightly around the expected level. Variance is not a directional signal — it is a measure of confidence in the estimate, not a prediction of which direction deviations will occur.

Variance and expected scoring are distinct concepts. A game can have a low expected total and high variance, or a high expected total and relatively tight variance. The Oracle does not conflate them.

Contributors to high run variance include:

- **Bullpen uncertainty:** When key bullpen arms are unavailable or in uncertain status, the post-starter run environment may swing substantially depending on which arms actually appear. An uncertain bullpen situation widens the scoring distribution even when the expected run total appears well-defined.
- **Command variability:** Starting pitchers with inconsistent command — those who may dominate for five innings or struggle through three, without a clear predictor for a given night — contribute to high variance. When command is volatile, the inning-by-inning run-scoring picture may be very different from the expected baseline.
- **Weather:** Extreme or rapidly changing conditions — wind shifts, temperature swings, mid-game precipitation — introduce run-scoring uncertainty that cannot be fully assessed before first pitch. Weather-driven variance is highest in outdoor venues with unsettled pre-game conditions.
- **Home-run dependence:** When the Over case is heavily concentrated in a small number of potential home-run events, the actual outcome depends on a few high-impact at-bats. This creates high variance around a moderate expected total — the game either stays well under or jumps well over depending on a handful of plate appearances.
- **Offensive consistency:** Lineups with highly consistent offensive profiles — steady contact hitters who generate traffic reliably — produce narrower scoring distributions than feast-or-famine offenses that either explode for eight runs or go quietly through the order. Inconsistent offenses increase variance around the expected scoring level.

The Oracle uses variance awareness to inform the confidence of its recommendation. A high-variance game produces a wider range of plausible total outcomes. When the expected run estimate is close to the posted total, high variance compounds the push-zone risk discussed in Section 6.8. The Oracle requires greater distance between its run-environment estimate and the posted line when variance is high, precisely because the uncertainty around the expected level makes a small edge less actionable.

**Team offensive interaction effects:**

The run environment in a baseball game does not emerge from two isolated team projections that are then combined. One team's offensive approach can materially influence the scoring conditions for both teams — not just for itself. The Oracle accounts for these interaction effects in the holistic synthesis.

- **Longer innings and pitch-count escalation:** A lineup that works deep counts — drawing walks, fighting through long at-bats, fouling off pitches — extends the duration of each inning and accelerates the opposing pitcher's pitch count. A starting pitcher facing a patient, count-working lineup accumulates pitch count faster than his typical pace, accelerating his exit and expanding the bullpen window earlier than expected. This affects the total run environment for both teams, because an earlier-than-expected starter exit exposes both teams' offenses to longer bullpen phases — which may be stronger or weaker than the starter depending on each team's bullpen situation.
- **Earlier bullpen exposure:** When one team's offense forces an early starter exit, both teams experience increased bullpen innings. If Team A's patient lineup drives Team B's starter out in the fourth inning, Team A faces Team B's bullpen for five innings instead of two — amplifying or suppressing Team A's total run potential depending on that bullpen's quality. Simultaneously, the early exit may trigger reciprocal managerial decisions that affect Team B's scoring environment in subsequent innings.
- **Defensive and pitching fatigue:** An offense that generates consistently long innings — many pitches, many at-bats, many baserunner situations — may contribute to cumulative pitcher fatigue and reduced concentration for later innings. These effects are secondary and difficult to quantify, but they represent a mechanism through which one team's offensive style can gradually affect the opposing pitching staff's effectiveness over nine innings.
- **Game-state interaction:** The score at various points in the game affects managerial decisions for both teams. A team trailing by four runs in the sixth inning is more likely to see lower-leverage depth arms deployed by the opposing manager; a close game in the eighth will see high-leverage arms regardless of prior usage. One team's scoring pattern — shaped by its offensive approach — creates the game states that govern the opposing manager's deployment decisions for the remaining innings.

The Oracle accounts for these interaction effects by considering not only each team's isolated scoring projection but also the mechanisms through which each team's offensive approach influences the conditions under which the other team will score. A patient, pitch-count-intensive offense affects the run environment in ways that parallel team projections would not capture. The run environment is an emergent property of both teams in interaction — not the sum of two independently calculated expected scoring levels.

**Mathematical models:**

The Oracle does not reject mathematical run-environment models. Where a model's assumptions are documented, its inputs are supportable by the available evidence, material uncertainty is disclosed, its calibration has been evaluated against known benchmarks, and its performance has been validated through Oracle evaluations and audits, such a model may be used to inform the run-environment estimate. What the Oracle prohibits is artificial or unsupported precision from models whose assumptions cannot be substantiated or whose inputs are incomplete. This version of the playbook does not require or specify a particular run-environment model. As Oracle Totals evaluations accumulate and validated models are developed, future editions may incorporate more formal run-environment modeling tools.

## Article VII — Evidence Hierarchy

### Section 7.1 — Why Evidence Quality Matters

The full-game Totals market encompasses nine innings of interaction between pitching, offense, environment, and game state. More than any other Oracle market, Totals evaluation relies on a large volume of evidence across many categories. The quality and reliability of that evidence varies widely, and the Oracle's run-environment estimate must reflect that variation. High-confidence evidence that is current, confirmed, and skill-based warrants more weight in the synthesis than narrative, projected, or stale evidence.

### Section 7.2 — Confirmed and Specific Evidence

The highest-quality evidence for a Totals recommendation is information that:

- Has been officially announced or confirmed before the analysis is documented
- Is specific to the game, matchup, conditions, or personnel being analyzed
- Can be verified by reference to a named source or data feed

Examples of confirmed, specific evidence in the Totals context:

- Both starting pitchers officially confirmed by both teams with confirmed roles (starter versus opener)
- An officially announced batting order confirming lineup depth and construction
- Confirmed umpire assignment where umpire analysis is material
- Published meteorological data for the outdoor venue, including wind direction and speed
- Confirmed roof status for a retractable-roof venue
- Published bullpen availability reports or confirmed rest days for key relievers
- Confirmed doubleheader status or getaway game scheduling

### Section 7.3 — Projected and Plausible Evidence

Lower than confirmed evidence, but still analytically usable, is evidence that:

- Is not yet confirmed but is expected with reasonable basis (probable starters not formally announced, expected lineups not yet posted)
- Is drawn from established, documentable patterns relevant to the current situation (a pitcher's well-documented walk tendency; a bullpen with recent heavy usage)

Projected evidence must be flagged as projected. Where material to the Totals thesis — particularly when either starting pitcher, bullpen availability, key lineup hitters, or weather conditions are projected rather than confirmed — a Conditional Play structure is appropriate.

### Section 7.4 — Narrative and General Evidence

The weakest category of evidence includes:

- General narratives about team quality, recent momentum, or reputation
- Full-season aggregate offensive statistics without assessment of current lineup status and recent performance
- Pitcher win-loss records or ERA without skill-based decomposition
- General statements about bullpen "depth" without confirmed arm availability
- Motivational or schedule narratives without specific, behavioral support

The Totals context carries specific narrative risks: the temptation to substitute a general "this feels like a high-scoring game" or "both pitchers are elite so this should be low" for a grounded, multi-category run-environment analysis. The Oracle explicitly checks whether the evidence it is relying on is specifically about the full-game run environment or whether it is partial-game or directional reasoning applied without appropriate calibration.

### Section 7.5 — Evidence Conflicts

When Totals evidence points in different directions — for example, an excellent pitching matchup combined with a depleted and unavailable bullpen — the Oracle must address the conflict explicitly. The correct response is one of three:

1. Document both sides, explain how the stronger evidence outweighs the weaker in the run-environment synthesis, and proceed with the appropriate verdict.
2. Classify the recommendation as a Conditional Play pending additional confirmation.
3. Pass, because the conflicting evidence indicates insufficient analytical clarity for a full-game run-environment recommendation.

### Section 7.6 — Documenting Uncertainty

Before first pitch, the Oracle explicitly distinguishes:

- **Confirmed:** Officially announced or verifiable from a primary source.
- **Estimated:** Expected but not officially confirmed. Probable starters not formally announced, projected lineups, expected weather, estimated umpire tendencies, likely bullpen availability.
- **Unknown:** Material to the analysis but genuinely unavailable before first pitch. An undisclosed bullpen injury, an unannounced lineup change, an unconfirmed umpire assignment.

Documenting uncertainty strengthens the analytical record and supports appropriate Conditional Play structuring.

## Article VIII — Decision Framework

### Section 8.1 — Overview

The decision framework is the Oracle's structured process for moving from evidence assessment to an Over or Under verdict. It follows the Evaluation Lifecycle defined in the Oracle Evaluation Standard. The steps below describe how the Oracle approaches a Totals opportunity from first consideration to final verdict.

### Section 8.2 — Step 1: Identify the Candidate

A game enters Totals evaluation when the Oracle identifies a specific reason to assess the full-game run environment — typically a pitching matchup, environmental condition, bullpen situation, or pricing anomaly that suggests the expected run total may meaningfully differ from the posted line. Identifying a candidate is not a commitment.

Valid reasons to identify a Totals candidate:
- A specific, documentable run-prevention case based on both starters and both bullpens pointing toward the Under
- A specific, documentable run-creation case based on both lineups facing opposing pitching vulnerabilities pointing toward the Over
- A specific environmental modifier — park, weather, or altitude — that materially adjusts the expected run environment
- A documented bullpen fatigue or unavailability situation that expands the expected run-scoring window beyond what the posted total implies
- A market pricing anomaly relative to the Oracle's initial run-environment assessment

### Section 8.3 — Step 2: Assess Both Starting Pitchers

Per Section 6.1, the Oracle assesses both starters' quality, durability, expected innings coverage, and run-prevention contribution. This step establishes the pitching picture for the first portion of the game and informs the estimated handoff point to the bullpen.

### Section 8.4 — Step 3: Assess Both Bullpen Environments

Per Section 6.2, the Oracle assesses both bullpens' quality, availability, recent workload, and expected deployment. This step completes the pitching picture for the full nine innings.

### Section 8.5 — Step 4: Assess Both Offenses

Per Section 6.3, the Oracle assesses both full lineups' run-creation capability, depth, and construction. This step establishes the offensive contribution picture for both teams across the full game.

### Section 8.6 — Step 5: Evaluate Offensive-Pitching Interactions

Per Section 6.4, the Oracle evaluates the specific matchup interactions between each offense and the opposing pitching: pitch types, handedness dynamics, walk pressure, contact versus power profiles. This step modulates the pitching and offensive assessments from general quality to specific matchup-adjusted run-environment expectations.

### Section 8.7 — Step 6: Apply Run Environment Modifiers

Per Sections 6.5, 6.6, and 6.7, the Oracle applies the park, weather, umpire, and game context modifiers as common inputs to the combined run-environment picture.

### Section 8.8 — Step 7: Synthesize the Run-Environment Estimate

Per Section 6.9, the Oracle integrates the full body of evidence into a single, honest run-environment assessment — acknowledging the directional conclusion, the strength of the supporting evidence, the primary risks, and the uncertainty inherent in the estimate.

### Section 8.9 — Step 8: Confirm Market Appropriateness

The Oracle confirms that the full-game Over or Under market is the most appropriate vehicle for its run-environment thesis. If a directional, F5, or first-inning market better expresses the underlying analytical position, the Oracle evaluates that market under its applicable playbook or passes without substitution.

### Section 8.10 — Step 9: Evaluate the Market Price

The Oracle compares its run-environment estimate to the implied probability in the available Over or Under price, accounting for the vig. It determines whether the available price represents value, is neutral, or is unfavorable.

### Section 8.11 — Step 10: Issue a Verdict

**Play:** The Oracle's run-environment estimate meaningfully differs from the market-implied probability in a documentable direction, all material conditions are confirmed, the full-game Totals market is the appropriate vehicle, and the available price offers value. The recommendation is activated.

**Conditional Play:** The directional assessment is clear, but one or more material conditions are unconfirmed before analysis is completed. Common Conditional Play triggers in this market: SP confirmation, lineup confirmation for a key hitter, bullpen availability confirmation, weather confirmation for an outdoor venue, roof status confirmation, or price threshold. The Oracle documents the specific conditions that must be confirmed before activation. Unmet conditions before first pitch result in a Void outcome.

**Pass:** The evidence is insufficient to support a clear run-environment assessment, the available evidence points in conflicting directions without a principled resolution, the price does not offer value, another market is more appropriate, or one or more pass conditions from Article X are triggered. Pass is always the correct outcome when qualifying evidence is absent.

## Article IX — Edge Identification Principles

### Section 9.1 — What Constitutes Identifiable Totals Edge

A Totals edge exists when the Oracle's estimated run environment differs meaningfully from the probability implied by the posted total and associated price, and when that difference is based on specific, documentable evidence assessed across the full nine-inning scope.

The Oracle does not need certainty. It needs to articulate specifically what the market has mispriced in the full-game run-environment context — and that articulation must be grounded in the evidence categories in Article VI.

### Section 9.2 — Common Sources of Totals Edge

The following are durable categories of analytical advantage that may appear in Totals markets:

**Mispriced bullpen fatigue:** The market has not adequately adjusted the posted total to account for confirmed bullpen unavailability or documented fatigue on one or both sides. A bullpen in significantly worse shape than the market's posted total implies creates an Under-expanding run window that may represent Over value.

**Overlooked weather or park effects:** A specific environmental condition — wind direction, extreme temperature, altitude effect — that interacts specifically with the pitching or offensive profiles in ways the market has underweighted. Park factor and wind direction are well-documented but may not be fully reflected in every posted total.

**Lineup news:** A confirmed change to a key middle-of-the-order hitter — late scratch, confirmed injury, lineup restructuring — is a material development that the Totals market may not immediately reflect. The impact on full-game offensive depth from a key absence may represent Under value.

**Market overreaction to pitching reputation:** The market has priced the total aggressively low based on the reputation of one or both starting pitchers without adequately accounting for the bullpen picture, the opposing offense's specific matchup advantages, or the run-environment conditions.

**Market overreaction to recent high- or low-scoring games:** A team's recent results have caused the market to overprice or underprice their run environment beyond what current pitching, offensive, and environmental evidence supports.

**Run environment mismatch:** The posted total reflects an average or neutral environmental assumption, but the confirmed conditions — an extreme park, confirmed wind direction, roof status — warrant a meaningful adjustment that the posted line has not incorporated.

**Hidden run-environment changes:** A schedule-driven situation — a bullpen taxed by an extra-inning game the night before, a starter moved up or pushed back creating an unexpected opener scenario — has changed the true run environment significantly from what the posted total assumes.

### Section 9.3 — Edge and Value Must Coexist

An Over or Under analytical edge and an acceptable market price are both required for a recommendation. Neither alone is sufficient. A strong run-environment conviction without an acceptable price is a pass.

### Section 9.4 — What Is Not a Totals Edge

The following are common sources of misidentified Totals edge:

- **A single dominant starting pitcher:** An elite starter on one team does not automatically support an Under. The full-game run environment requires assessing both pitching staffs across nine innings and both offenses. One pitcher — however excellent — is not the complete run-environment picture.
- **Recent game results without structural support:** A team that has been in three consecutive low-scoring games is not automatically involved in an Under. The Oracle evaluates the structural run-environment factors — current pitching quality, bullpen status, offensive profile, environmental conditions — not recent results alone.
- **General "offensive" or "pitching" narratives:** A vague narrative that "this is a pitching-dominant game" or "these are two explosive offenses" without specific, documentable evidence grounded in the evaluation categories is not a Totals edge.
- **Full-game total as a directional substitute:** Using the Totals market as an alternative expression of a directional thesis that belongs in the moneyline is not appropriate. The Oracle evaluates the full-game run environment specifically, not the directional outcome.
- **Team total reasoning extended to game total:** A strong case that one team will score under three runs does not automatically translate into a game Under. The opposing team's scoring must be assessed independently.

## Article X — Pass Conditions

Each of the following conditions independently requires a pass on the Over or Under market. A triggered pass condition requires a Pass regardless of how strong the case appears in other dimensions.

### Section 10.1 — No Clear Run-Environment Assessment

The Oracle cannot articulate a specific, documentable basis for believing the expected run environment meaningfully differs from the market-implied total. A general directional preference without evidence grounded in the full-game evaluation categories does not constitute a Totals thesis.

### Section 10.2 — Either Starting Pitcher Unconfirmed

The Oracle's run-environment estimate depends critically on both starting pitchers' identities, quality, and expected roles. An unconfirmed starting pitcher — particularly uncertainty about whether a traditional starter or an opener will begin the game — introduces material uncertainty into the pitching picture. Where confirmation is expected before first pitch, a Conditional Play structure is appropriate. Where confirmation is unlikely before first pitch, the recommendation is a pass.

### Section 10.3 — Material Lineup Uncertainty

A key middle-of-the-order or deep-lineup contributor is unconfirmed in a situation where a late scratch is materially possible. For a full-lineup Totals analysis, significant lineup uncertainty — particularly regarding power hitters or depth contributors whose presence materially affects the full-game offensive assessment — constitutes material analytical risk.

### Section 10.4 — Weather or Environmental Uncertainty

At an outdoor venue, precipitation risk, wind conditions, temperature extremes, or other weather factors materially affecting the full-game run environment are uncertain in a way that cannot be resolved before first pitch. At a retractable-roof venue, roof status is unconfirmed where it is material. Significant precipitation risk also creates game postponement risk, which would void the Totals market.

### Section 10.5 — Material Bullpen Uncertainty

The availability of key bullpen arms — particularly in situations where recent heavy usage makes arm availability material to the run-environment assessment — cannot be confirmed before first pitch. An unresolved bullpen availability uncertainty that materially affects the full-game pitching picture is a Totals pass consideration.

### Section 10.6 — Insufficient Full-Game Run-Environment Evidence

The Oracle lacks adequate evidence to assess the run environment for the full nine innings. Partial analysis — only the starting pitching picture, or only the first-five-innings frame — is insufficient for a full-game Totals recommendation.

### Section 10.7 — Sportsbook Settlement Uncertainty

The applicable sportsbook's settlement rules for this game's Over/Under market are unclear for a plausible scenario — specifically regarding extra-inning play, suspended game resolution, or rain-shortened game treatment — and the settlement treatment could materially affect the recommendation's validity.

### Section 10.8 — Conflicting Evidence Without Resolution

The full-game run-environment picture presents evidence pointing meaningfully in both directions — for example, excellent starting pitching across both sides combined with severely depleted bullpens — in a way that cannot be resolved by principled weighing of one side's evidence against the other. The correct outcome is a pass.

### Section 10.9 — Price Has Moved Beyond Acceptable Threshold

The Over or Under price has moved to a level where the Oracle's run-environment estimate no longer supports a positive-expected-value recommendation. A directional assessment that was valid at the opening price does not automatically remain valid after line movement.

### Section 10.10 — Daily Volume Limit Would Be Exceeded

Activating the recommendation would exceed the daily play limit established by the Tracking Rules. Constitutional Rule 1 (No Forced Plays) and Tracking Rule 4 govern this condition. A compelling run-environment case does not override a volume limit.

### Section 10.11 — Pregame Documentation Cannot Be Completed

The Oracle cannot complete all required pregame fields, all Core Evaluation Category assessments, and the Process Score before first pitch. Incomplete pregame documentation at first pitch is a failed-process record (Process Score: F). If the Oracle cannot document the play properly before first pitch, it does not make the play.

## Article XI — Common Analytical Errors

The following errors represent the most frequent ways full-game Totals analysis goes wrong. The Oracle must be able to recognize them in its own reasoning and correct them before a recommendation is made.

### Section 11.1 — Starting Pitcher Tunnel Vision

Evaluating the full-game run environment primarily through the lens of starting pitcher quality, while neglecting the bullpen contribution, full-lineup offensive depth, and environmental modifiers. Both starting pitchers are only one component of the nine-inning run picture. A game with two elite starters and two depleted bullpens may be a genuine Over; a game with two mediocre starters and two excellent bullpens may be a genuine Under. Starting pitcher quality alone does not determine the full-game Totals verdict.

### Section 11.2 — Bullpen Neglect

Making a Totals recommendation without adequately assessing the bullpen environment on one or both sides. Because both bullpens will pitch three to five innings in most games, their run-prevention quality, availability, and fatigue are primary Totals factors — not secondary ones. Bullpen neglect is the Totals-specific version of the most common full-game run-environment error.

### Section 11.3 — Weather Narrative Overreach

Building a Totals recommendation primarily on weather or environmental conditions without a well-grounded underlying pitching and offensive interaction analysis. Environmental conditions are probability modifiers applied to a grounded run-environment assessment — not substitutes for it. "The wind is blowing out" is not a standalone Over argument.

### Section 11.4 — Park Inflation

Overweighting park factor as a run-environment driver without accounting for the specific pitching profiles and offensive compositions of the teams involved. A high-run-environment park suppresses the Under case, but not all games at that park are Overs. A pitcher who induces ground balls is less affected by a fly-ball-suppressive park effect than a fly-ball pitcher. The Oracle applies park factor as a specific modifier, not as a universal dictate.

### Section 11.5 — Offensive Reputation Bias

Anchoring the run-environment assessment to a team's or player's public offensive reputation without verifying that the reputation applies to current-season performance, current lineup construction, and the specific pitching matchup. Offensive reputations are aggregate historical signals; current run-environment contribution depends on the specific game context.

### Section 11.6 — Recent Score Anchoring

Allowing a team's or game's recent scoring results — a series of high-scoring or low-scoring games — to anchor the run-environment estimate without checking whether the structural factors that produced those results still apply. A team that has been involved in five consecutive Under games is not more likely to produce another Under unless the structural run-environment factors that drove those results are still present in this matchup.

### Section 11.7 — Ignoring Price

Making a Totals directional assessment without explicitly comparing the available Over or Under price to the Oracle's run-environment probability estimate. A strong run-environment conviction at an unfavorable price is a pass, not a recommendation. The Oracle makes an explicit price-to-probability comparison for every Totals recommendation.

### Section 11.8 — Lineup Depth Neglect

Evaluating offensive run-creation capability for the full-game market using only top-of-order analysis. The Totals market resolves after nine innings in which the full lineup cycles through multiple times. The Oracle's offensive assessment for Totals explicitly includes the full lineup depth — not just hitters 1 through 3 or 1 through 5.

### Section 11.9 — Run Environment Double Counting

Counting a shared environmental factor — park, weather, altitude, roof status, umpire zone — as a supporting input for each team's half of the run-environment analysis rather than as a single common modifier applied once to the combined picture. Double-counting shared factors inflates the apparent run-environment case in either direction and is an analytical error. See also Section 6.9.

### Section 11.10 — Forcing Action

Making a Totals recommendation because a game appears interesting at first glance, because the Oracle has been inactive, or because one component — typically starting pitcher quality — looks favorable in isolation, without a complete, multi-category run-environment assessment. Constitutional Rule 1 (No Forced Plays) applies with full force. The Totals market's intuitive appeal — "these are two bad pitching teams, of course it goes Over" — makes it particularly susceptible to forced plays based on incomplete analysis.

## Article XII — Relationship to the Evaluation Standard

### Section 12.1 — How This Playbook Serves the Five Evaluation Categories

The Oracle Evaluation Standard's five pregame evaluation categories apply to every Oracle recommendation, including all Totals plays. This playbook provides the full-game run-environment-specific analytical content that informs those categories.

---

**Game Selection** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.1)

The Oracle's decision to engage a specific game with an Over or Under recommendation is informed by the Core Evaluation Categories in Article VI. A game merits Totals evaluation when an initial review identifies a specific, articulable run-environment basis — a compelling pitching picture, a confirmed bullpen situation, an environmental condition, or a pricing signal — that suggests the posted total may be meaningfully mispriced. A game evaluated and found to lack a documentable run-environment thesis results in a pass — a correct Game Selection outcome.

---

**Market Selection** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.2)

The Oracle's decision to use the full-game Over or Under rather than another market is governed by Article IV (When the Market Is Appropriate) and Article V (When Another Market Is Preferable). A recommendation that uses the full-game Totals market when a directional, F5, or first-inning market better expressed the thesis is a Market Selection weakness. The selection of Over versus Under — after the game has been identified as a Totals candidate — is also a Market Selection decision governed by the run-environment synthesis in Section 6.9 and the edge identification framework in Article IX.

---

**Handicap Quality** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.3)

The Oracle's analytical work — its assessment of both starting pitchers, both bullpen environments, both full lineups, the offensive-pitching interactions, the environmental modifiers, and the holistic run-environment synthesis — constitutes its Totals handicapping. Strong Handicap Quality requires full-game evidence covering all nine innings, honest engagement with the bullpen picture, complete lineup analysis, appropriate environmental adjustment, and a clearly stated directional thesis. Weak Handicap Quality results from starting-pitcher-only analysis, bullpen neglect, top-of-order-only lineup analysis, or environmental narrative without pitching and offensive grounding.

---

**Value and Price** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.4)

The Oracle's assessment of the Over or Under price, relative to its run-environment estimate, is governed by Section 6.8 (Market Context and Pricing) and Article VIII (Decision Framework). A recommendation that accepted an unfavorable price without documentation, that failed to account for the sportsbook hold, that relied on alternate total pricing from a different market, or that compared the available price to a moneyline or F5 closing price rather than the Oracle's full-game run-environment estimate is a Value and Price weakness.

---

**Execution and Discipline** (Evaluation Standard Section 4.5)

The Oracle's compliance with Constitutional Rules, Tracking Rules, Totals pass conditions (Article X), and documentation requirements governs the Execution and Discipline category. A Totals recommendation that activates on an unconfirmed starting pitcher without a Conditional Play structure, fails to complete a full nine-inning bullpen assessment, neglects full-lineup depth analysis, enters a game with material weather uncertainty without acknowledging void risk, fails to complete all pregame fields before first pitch, or violates the daily volume limit is an Execution and Discipline failure.

### Section 12.2 — CLV for Full-Game Totals

Closing Line Value (CLV) for Over and Under bets is calculated using market-specific price comparisons:

**Over CLV:** Compare the Oracle's Over entry price to the closing Over price on the same full-game total market. The closing price must be drawn from the same market — not from the Under side, not from a moneyline, not from an F5 total, and not from a team total.

**Under CLV:** Compare the Oracle's Under entry price to the closing Under price on the same full-game total market. The same market-specific comparison requirement applies.

CLV for Totals does not involve spread adjustment. The market is a single Over/Under line with associated juice.

At minimum, CLV tracking for Totals requires:
- The Oracle's entry price (Over or Under direction) recorded at the time of recommendation activation
- The closing price for the same market and direction
- A directional comparison: whether the Oracle's entry price was more or less favorable than the closing price
- When a valid closing price cannot be obtained — because the market was taken off the board or data cannot be verified — the closing price field is recorded as Unavailable per the Tracker Standard status markers; the entry price is still recorded

### Note on Future Evolution

This playbook establishes the Oracle's foundational Totals methodology. It defines the analytical structure for the most comprehensive wagering window this Oracle evaluates — one that requires assessment of both starting pitchers, both bullpens, both full lineups, environmental modifiers, and market pricing across all nine innings. As Oracle Totals evaluations accumulate and milestone audits generate validated findings, future editions may refine the analytical categories and deepen the interaction frameworks.

The following represent possible areas for future analytical enhancement. Each requires validation through Oracle Totals evaluations and audit findings before any inclusion in this playbook:

- **Weather and environmental models:** As Oracle evaluations accumulate in varying weather conditions, it may become possible to develop more formal probability models for the run-environment impact of specific wind directions, temperature bands, and humidity ranges — particularly in their interaction with specific park dimensions, pitching profiles, and lineup power compositions.
- **Run expectancy models:** Formal models for estimating expected runs per inning given specific lineup constructions, pitcher profiles, and game states may improve the Oracle's run-environment estimation. Whether such models meet the evidence quality and validation standards required by this playbook is a question for future Oracle evaluation.
- **Park interaction models:** Beyond aggregate park factors, more granular models for how specific park attributes — wall distances, foul territory, playing surface — interact with specific pitcher and hitter profiles may improve the park-adjustment precision in the run-environment synthesis.
- **Bullpen usage models:** Systematic models for estimating bullpen innings coverage and run-prevention quality given documented arm availability, recent workload, and managerial deployment tendencies may improve the post-starter run-environment assessment.
- **Offensive clustering models:** Models that estimate not only expected total offensive output but also the probability distribution of how runs cluster into multi-run innings — which affects Over/Under probability at specific totals — may improve the Oracle's price-to-probability comparison.
- **Scoring distribution models:** Rather than estimating a single expected run total, a scoring distribution model would estimate the probability of various total run outcomes, enabling a more precise comparison to the posted total and associated price. Development of such models requires significant Oracle evaluation data and validation.
- **Monte Carlo simulation:** A simulation approach that models the full-game run environment through repeated sampling of per-inning run production distributions may allow more sophisticated expected-value calculations for specific Over/Under totals. Such an approach requires formally specified input distributions and extensive calibration before it would meet Oracle validation standards.
- **AI probability calibration:** As the Oracle accumulates a meaningful sample of Totals evaluations with documented predictions and outcomes, machine-learning calibration of the Oracle's probability estimates against realized outcomes may improve systematic biases in the run-environment assessment methodology.

All amendments to this playbook are subject to the Constitutional Amendment Procedure defined in Oracle Constitution Article VI. No analytical observation, however compelling, modifies this playbook without Constitution Authority approval through that procedure. Evolution is continuous; it is also governed.
